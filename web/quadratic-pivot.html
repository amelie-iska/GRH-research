Geometry and capacity

Aⱼ are dyadic norm scales, not six sampled ideals. Capacity is checked for these scales; fixed annular constants are suppressed. Globally r₆ is arbitrary and may overlap other factors. A₆ = 1 is a choice of this slice, not a global squarefree or coprimality restriction.

WHAT THE INPUT DOES — AND WHAT IS LEFT

A classical theorem, applied in the original family.

Goldmakher–Louvel, Corollary 1.2, applies to the cube of a sixth-order Hecke family: n = 6, power n/2 = 3. The quadratic pivot is the factor r₃³ in the original row, with the other five factors frozen.

The original collision zeros stay in the characters. Fixed local phases are handled by a finite partition; restrictions on the nonnegative outer row sum can then be relaxed. No arbitrary coupled mask is deleted.

Quadratic: Λ ≲ (HD)ε [R + DR/A₃]
Old: Λ ≲ (HD)ε [R + DR/M + D2/3R/M1/3]
R = ∏ⱼ Aⱼ · M = max(A₁,A₂)

On this face, the old leading power is always 5/4, while the quadratic power is 1 + 2a. Taking the better power gives min(5/4, 1 + 2a). The strict-saving sector is exactly 0 ≤ a < 1/8.

At a = 1/8 and throughout the remaining half-face, the available leading power stays 5/4. Even the best point, a = 0, gives power 1, above the target 3/4. The original signed common-divisor-one coupled sum, uniform neighboring blocks and the arithmetic determinant bridge remain open.

This is a refined sector bound using an external theorem. It is not a new large-sieve theorem, a global exponent improvement, a new Lean result or a proof of GRH.
