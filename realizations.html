01

One interface, three explicit layers.

A realization needs a map from the graph model to an admissible operation and a measurable physical response. Each layer preserves different information.

Proposed interface
QUANTUM STATE

Admissible operations

Specify a density matrix, its operator algebra and the available channels. Classical location with quantum internal states uses a direct sum; coherence between locations requires off-diagonal blocks in a larger algebra.

GRAPH CONTROL

Tropical routing

Positive route weights admit logarithmic bounds and max-plus comparisons. Complex amplitudes also carry phase: a largest-path envelope does not recover interference or the sum of all paths.

PHYSICAL MODEL

Measured realization

Declare the substrate, encoding, controls, readout and operating domain. A fluid interpretation also needs physical scales, conservation laws, closure assumptions and a controlled approximation.

For every fixed setting, a completely positive, trace-preserving channel is linear in its input density matrix. Nonlinearity can enter through a classical controller, conditioning on a measured outcome, or a stated mean-field approximation. These mechanisms carry different physical obligations.

The reviewed ToricGT code supplies graph tokens, finite Weyl features and a mixture of ordinary and max-plus aggregation. A physical compiler must check the algebraic relations it preserves; equal scalar path counts do not make noncommuting quantum operations interchangeable.

04

Generate a candidate. Measure its operator.

Material generation and device-geometry optimization address different stages. Both need a physical forward model and an independently checked realization target.

Proposed design pipeline

  1. Define the target and its cost

    Specify the quiver, retained relations, state space, observables, error tolerance and resource budget. Decide whether the task needs nonclassical quantum processing or a classical wave response.

  2. Propose a material or geometry

    Condition a generator on the required properties and hard constraints. Preserve the model version and seeds. MatterGen supplies a crystal-generation precedent; physics-driven generative photonics supplies a geometry-search precedent.

  3. Evaluate the appropriate physics

    Model the declared regime, boundary conditions, loss and uncertainty. Check conservation or channel admissibility, accessible controls and reduced-model error. A predicted material property is not a demonstrated quantum channel.

  4. Design and certify the controller

    Fit the task on declared data, preserve held-out conditions, and justify a gain envelope. Independently replay the finite witness and propagate solver, fabrication and readout errors through its budget.

  5. Calibrate, test and compare

    An actual experimental study must measure the encoding, operation and readout, including drift. Compare with conventional solvers and simpler learning systems at equal accuracy, charging preparation, measurement, reset and controller costs.

A simulator-only result remains a simulator result. The cited generative methods do not establish a ToricGT implementation, a universal quantum device or a guaranteed global design optimum.
