# Independent mathematical review of the gentle and determinant chapters

Reviewed on 13 November 2026. Scope: the complete `gentle-invariants.tex`
chapter pair, including its original elementary arguments. This review does
not turn the cited external theorems into independently verified proofs or
Lean-certified statements.

## Arguments checked

- The three-vertex complex decomposition and its rank restrictions, the
  one-dimensional reducible example, and the forbidden-path color construction.
- The Kronecker examples, regular Jordan-block endomorphisms, determinant
  pencil, and distinction between regular parameters and other module types.
- The radical-square-zero oriented triangle and its periodic projective
  resolution.
- The Schofield map, kernel, extension cokernel, determinant nonvanishing
  criterion, point-action character, and the sign reversal for the action on
  coordinate functions. The bound-quiver paragraph correctly requires a
  genuine injective two-term projective resolution.
- The highest-weight two-factor determinant-character condition.
- The corrected Carroll–Weyman Example 8.20 presentation: nonnegative
  generator reduction, the complete integer kernel, uniqueness of reduced
  monomials, both generating binomial relations, dimension six, and normality
  by intersection of the group algebra with the polynomial ring. In
  particular, the first relation contains **B1**, not B2.
- Exterior-trace expansion, exact-sequence determinant multiplicativity,
  cancellation through cohomology, formal logarithms, and the primitive
  closed-walk Euler product.
- The elliptic curve over F17: 19 rational points, trace −1, zeta numerator
  `1 + t + 17t²`, reciprocal-root modulus, and the 323-point quadratic
  extension count. The manuscript correctly retains the external status of
  the general Weil/cohomological theorems.

## Source concordance

The local primary-source text for Carroll–Weyman, *Semi-invariants for gentle
string algebras*, arXiv:1106.0774v1, confirms Theorem 6.10 is an isomorphism
of actual rings, not merely an associated-graded identification. Its Claim 3
asserts the multiplicative identification used in the chapter. Example 8.20
on printed page 40 contains B2 in the first displayed relation, which does
not agree with the displayed monomial generators; the manuscript's corrected
B1 relation agrees with their exponent vectors. Corollaries 9.2–9.3 support
the stated generator/relation degree bounds. The sum is over arrows, as in
the source's proof and introduction.

Carroll–Chindris, *On the invariant theory for acyclic gentle algebras*,
arXiv:1210.3579v2, Theorem 1, agrees with the chapter's characteristic-zero,
acyclicity, regular-component, nonempty semistability, and regular stable
summand qualifications. Its rational-invariant transcendence-degree statement
counts regular indecomposable components with their generic multiplicities.

Sources: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0774> and
<https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3579>. The review checked the relevant theorem
statements and hypotheses against these supplied local primary documents;
it did not re-prove all of their external classification or invariant-theory
results.

## Two precision edits

1. The relation ideal is **generated by** monochromatic consecutive
   length-two compositions. Saying that the ideal consists precisely of
   these paths omits their ideal multiples and linear combinations.
2. The virtual determinant in (Z3) uses the Grothendieck group of
   finite-dimensional spaces **equipped with endomorphisms**, with relations
   from equivariant short exact sequences. The ordinary Grothendieck group
   of vector spaces only records dimension and cannot determine this
   characteristic rational function.

No other mathematical correction was identified in this bounded review.
