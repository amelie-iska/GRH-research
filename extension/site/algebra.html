01

Six minors, one relation.

The two rows of a 2 × 4 matrix determine an exterior product. Its six coordinates obey a quadratic relation.

Edit the matrix

Enter integers from −9 to 9. Columns are numbered 1–4.

pij = m1im2j − m1jm2i, for i < j.

Exterior coordinates

p12p34 − p13p24 + p14p23 = 0

Why this identity matters

Expanding the three products cancels every term in pairs. The result is a polynomial identity over every commutative ring. A nonzero exterior product gives a projective point of Gr(2,4) over a field; zero minors give the zero exterior product, which is not a projective point. The displayed row rank is computed over ℚ and can change after reduction modulo a prime.

Straightening rewrites products that violate a chosen tableau order using relations such as this one. A basis theorem also needs a proof of independence, beyond the ability to rewrite expressions.

Source: manuscript, “Two-row tableaux and the Plücker algebra.” Read the proof.

Hook lengths and characteristic assumptions

The hook–content product is ∏(i,j)∈λ(3+j−i)/hij. For shape (3,2), the hook lengths are 4, 3, 1 in the first row and 2, 1 in the second; the corresponding contents plus 3 are 3, 4, 5 and 2, 3. Thus 360/24 = 15 as an integer identity.

The integral Schur module has this tableau basis; its rank remains 15 after base change, including to positive characteristic. Irreducibility and semisimple representation decompositions are additional characteristic-zero results. Dividing by the hook product 24 inside a field of characteristic 2 or 3 is not a valid substitute for the integral construction.

Source: manuscript, “Partitions and a characteristic-free Schur presentation,” including the Akin–Buchsbaum–Weyman basis theorem. Read the theorem and qualifications.

03

A relation determines the modules.

For the quiver 1 → 2 → 3 with ba = 0, a representation is a complex kp → kq → kr. Its two ranks determine its decomposition over any field.

Allowable rank pairs

0 ≤ u ≤ min(p,q), 0 ≤ v ≤ min(q,r), and u+v ≤ q. Select a pair to change the decomposition.

Inspect U, V, and the zero composite

In this chosen basis, U has an identity in its first u positions. V uses the next v middle coordinates, so VU is exactly zero. Zero-dimensional spaces are allowed.

The multiplicities are M₁₂ᵘ ⊕ M₂₃ᵛ ⊕ S₁ᵖ⁻ᵘ ⊕ S₂ᑫ⁻ᵘ⁻ᵛ ⊕ S₃ʳ⁻ᵛ. Choosing bases of im U, a complement in ker V, and a complement to ker V proves this decomposition. Each of these five modules has endomorphism ring k and is indecomposable.

Source: manuscript, “Complete module decomposition for the two-arrow relation.” The (1,2,1) equation is x₁y₁+x₂y₂=0: explore its four-dimensional real cone.

Why reduction terminates and the correction is necessary

Each rewrite removes two Y factors or two Z factors. The right sides create neither Y nor Z, so both reductions terminate and commute. A reduced monomial contains neither both Y₁,Y₂ nor both Z₁,Z₂. The integer kernel calculation in the manuscript proves that two reduced monomials with the same ten-coordinate vector are identical; this establishes uniqueness beyond the displayed finite examples.

The grading comes from the underlying variables: deg Xᵢ = 2, deg Yᵢ = 4, deg Zᵢ = 3, deg B₁ = 4, deg B₂ = 2. The relations have degrees 8 and 6. Treating all eight generator symbols as degree one would not preserve these relations.

Primary source: Carroll–Weyman, Semi-Invariants for Gentle String Algebras, Example 8.20. Correction and full normal-form proof: manuscript, “A matching semigroup with two explicit binomial relations.”
