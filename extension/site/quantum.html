Read the ansatz, derivative, and scope
For this encoded state and this star, the analytic reference is fθ(s) = (∏ᵢsᵢ)(∏ᵢcos θᵢ). The worker still applies every gate to all 2¹⁸ amplitudes and computes the readout from those amplitudes. The reference identity provides an independent check; it is not substituted for execution.
Each rotation parameter appears once. Its expectation derivative is [f(θ + πeⱼ/2) − f(θ − πeⱼ/2)]/2. The loss gradient then uses 2(f − y) times that derivative, averaged over the four examples. Shifting the squared loss directly would give a different and generally incorrect gradient.
The initial angles already give the correct prediction signs on all sixteen inputs. Optimization calibrates their magnitudes toward ±1 and improves mean squared error; it does not improve classification accuracy from a lower baseline. These four parameters are not uniquely identifiable from their cosine product.
This is a small variational quantum model on an encoded surface-code state, with a deliberately transparent target. Successful training does not establish a quantum learning advantage, fault tolerance of the ansatz, or general performance on external datasets. The variational gates may leave the code space. No hardware noise model or finite-shot sampling is added.

What the connection establishes
A clean dessin supplies a finite oriented map. Its vertices, edges and faces define a chain complex over 𝔽₂, and the vanishing boundary composition gives commuting CSS checks. The resulting quantum code carries k = 2g logical qubits on a connected closed orientable surface. The site computes this combinatorial structure and executes the specified circuits. It does not construct an explicit Belyi map, prove a new fault-tolerance threshold, or resolve an analytic Yang–Mills question.
Background: Kitaev, fault-tolerant quantum computation by anyons · Schuld et al., evaluating analytic gradients on quantum hardware · the research monograph and its evidence boundaries.
