LABORATORY 01 Arithmetic → geometry

A lattice becomes a torus.

Identify opposite sides of a period parallelogram. The quotient remembers the lattice’s complex structure even when the familiar embedded torus has the same shape.

L = ℤ + ℤτ   ·   E(ℂ) ≅ ℂ/L   ·   z = u + vτ

Current arithmetic

Flat area
Gram matrix
Seam residual

What the embedding means. The displayed map is T(u,v)=((2+0.7 cos 2πv) cos 2πu, (2+0.7 cos 2πv) sin 2πu, 0.7 sin 2πv). It visualizes the quotient topology. It does not preserve the flat metric or complex structure of ℂ/L. The seam residual measures periodicity of this embedding.

Read the mathematical connection

A holomorphic group map ℂ/L → ℂ/M lifts to a map z ↦ αz with αL ⊆ M. Thus End(ℂ/L) is the ring of complex scalars preserving L. It is ℤ or an order in an imaginary quadratic field. The manuscript proves this elementary dichotomy and distinguishes it from the quaternion endomorphism algebra of a supersingular curve in positive characteristic.

Sources: manuscript, “Elliptic curves through their endomorphism lattices”; Sutherland, complex multiplication.

LABORATORY 02 Algebra → four-dimensional geometry

The equation that makes a complex.

A column x followed by a row y composes to zero exactly when x·y=0. Its cone in four real coordinates has a torus as its unit link. Rank changes are visible in the same model.

ℝ —x→ ℝ² —yᵀ→ ℝ   ·   yᵀx = 0

Composition residual

Ranks (d₀, d₁)

Exact construction, numerical display. Set u=(cos α,sin α)/√2 and v=(cos β,sin β)/√2, then x=ρ(u+v)/√2 and y=ρ(u−v)/√2. Thus x·y=0 and ‖x‖²+‖y‖²=ρ². All diagnostics use these original coordinates. The subsequent 4D rotations change the viewing basis; they are not asserted to preserve the equation in the original coordinate split. Floating-point ranks use tolerance 10⁻¹⁰.

Why the torus appears, and what rank tells us

The orthogonal coordinate change (x,y) ↦ ((x+y)/√2,(x−y)/√2) converts x·y=0 into ‖u‖²=‖v‖². On the unit sphere, both squared lengths are 1/2, leaving an independent circle in each plane. For the displayed three-term complex, dim H¹=2−rank x−rank y. The alternating sum dim H⁰−dim H¹+dim H² stays zero, including at the cone vertex.

Source: manuscript, varieties of complexes and finite-dimensional Hodge theory. The displayed two-vector case is proved directly; it does not visualize every Buchsbaum–Eisenbud variety.

LABORATORY 03 Arithmetic → a finite group

Nineteen points, one cyclic group.

Over a finite field, an elliptic curve is a finite collection of residue pairs together with the point at infinity. Repeated addition of P visits every point in this example.

The computational question. Scalar multiplication takes k and computes [k]P. The discrete logarithm asks for k given P and [k]P. This nineteen-element group is an educational example. It makes no security claim about cryptographic parameter sizes.

All points and the addition formula

For distinct affine x-coordinates, λ=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁). For doubling with y≠0, λ=(3x₁²+2)/(2y₁). Then x₃=λ²−x₁−x₂ and y₃=λ(x₁−x₃)−y₁, all in 𝔽₁₇. Division means multiplication by a modular inverse. Inverse pairs sum to 𝒪, which acts as the identity.

LABORATORY 04 A hidden subgroup → linear information

Fourier sampling reveals an annihilator.

A uniform state on the kernel of f(a,b)=a+db transforms to a distribution on the characters that vanish on that kernel. The surviving line records d.

H = {(−dt, t) : t ∈ 𝔽₇}   ·   H⊥ = {(u, v) : v = du}

What is being simulated. The code evaluates a finite Fourier sum classically. It illustrates the ideal hidden-subgroup step with exact kernel states and a known modulus. It is not a quantum-device run, a complete implementation of Shor’s algorithm, or a resource estimate. A coset shift changes Fourier phases but leaves these probabilities unchanged.

Derive the support and recover the slope

The amplitude at (u,v) is 7⁻³ᐟ² Σₜ exp(2πi(v−du)t/7). The geometric sum is seven when v−du=0 and zero otherwise. Its squared magnitude is therefore 1/7 on H⊥ and zero elsewhere. A sample with u≠0 yields d=v/u modulo seven; the origin is uninformative and occurs with probability 1/7.

Source: manuscript, finite Fourier analysis and quantum discrete logarithms. The identity is a direct character-orthogonality calculation.

LABORATORY 05 Homological information → spectral limits

An index can stay fixed as an eigenvalue vanishes.

This finite complex has Euler index zero for every ε. Its middle Laplacian acquires an arbitrarily small eigenvalue, so the index alone supplies no positive lower spectral bound.

d₀ = (ε, 0)ᵀ   ·   d₁ = (0, 1)   ·   Δ₁ = diag(ε², 1)

Least eigenvalue
Smallest positive eigenvalue
dim ker Δ₁
Euler index
0

The distinction at zero. For ε>0, the smallest positive eigenvalue is ε². At ε=0, zero enters the spectrum, while the smallest strictly positive eigenvalue is one. The vanishing coercivity constant and the jump in the kernel are different statements. This example explains why an index theorem does not establish the Yang–Mills mass gap.

Read the finite-dimensional Hodge calculation

The composition d₁d₀ is zero. With standard Euclidean inner products, Δ₁=d₀d₀ᵀ+d₁ᵀd₁. Its quadratic form is ε²a²+b², so the kernel is zero for ε>0 and the first coordinate line for ε=0. In the latter case the cohomology dimensions are (1,1,0), whose alternating sum is still zero. An analytic elliptic complex requires differential operators and symbol exactness in addition to this algebraic chain relation.

Source: manuscript, Hodge theory, elliptic complexes, and the separation of index from coercivity.

LABORATORY 06 Local gauge choice → an invariant observable

A plaquette keeps its phase.

Changing the phase at a vertex changes the two adjacent edge descriptions. Their contributions cancel around the closed loop, leaving its U(1) holonomy unchanged.

Uᵢⱼ′ = exp(iλᵢ) Uᵢⱼ exp(−iλⱼ)   ·   W = U₀₁U₁₂U₂₃U₃₀

Scope of this model. This is one abelian plaquette, not a continuum limit or a nonabelian Yang–Mills construction. It demonstrates gauge cancellation exactly; the residual reports only floating-point arithmetic in the display.

Read the cancellation proof and edge data

Write Uᵢ,ᵢ₊₁=exp(i aᵢ). A gauge transformation changes aᵢ to aᵢ+λᵢ−λᵢ₊₁. Summing over the four cyclic edges cancels every vertex phase, so the product W=exp(i Σaᵢ) is invariant. For nonabelian groups the ordered product is conjugated by the gauge element at the base vertex; its trace is invariant.

Existence and the mass gap
are distinct milestones.

The Yang–Mills working group reports a Wightman quantum field theory from the four-dimensional Wilson lattice scaling limit. Its existence result was independently assessed on 10 November 2026. Uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit and the mass gap remain open.

This companion explains the reported result in its stated scope. It does not claim a solution of the Clay mass-gap problem or derive one from an index theorem.
