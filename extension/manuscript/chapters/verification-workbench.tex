\reviewstatus{This chapter supplies written arguments for finite interfaces
and identifies their selected Lean components. The companion exports exact

\section{What crosses the laboratory--proof boundary}

A laboratory displays a real-valued model through floating-point
approximations while a theorem quantifies over exact real numbers. Connecting
the two requires a precise interpretation of the controls. The workbench uses
reduced rational strings for exact parameters and canonical natural-number
strings for finite indices. An input \(1/3\) denotes that rational number;
its canvas image is an approximation. A logarithmic slider value such as
\(10^{-s}\) is not silently renamed as an exact rational approximation.

Each certificate carries a schema, a contract, exact parameters, a recomputable
result, and bindings to theorem names, source hashes, compiler-printed types,
actual axiom dependencies, runtime/model hashes and the pinned compiler. The
parser rejects duplicate keys, unknown fields, JSON numeric literals,
noncanonical fractions, excessive nesting and oversized inputs. These checks
protect the record's meaning. A content hash is not a signature.

A control adjustment immediately evaluates its finite exact condition.
Export preserves that state and its evidence boundary. Import validates the
complete record, recomputes its result, commits a reversible change and reads
back the controls; unsuccessful restoration rolls back. A separate operation
generates Lean source from fixed templates and validated literals. Checking
that file with the pinned compiler is the return path to the proof system.
The web page itself does not run a Lean kernel.

\section{Exact algebraic boundaries}

\begin{theorem}[The displayed Hodge family]\label{thm:workbench-hodge-contract}
For \(0\leq\varepsilon\leq1\), let
\[
 H_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad E_\varepsilon(a,b)=\varepsilon^2a^2+b^2.
\]
Then \(E_\varepsilon(a,b)\geq\varepsilon^2(a^2+b^2)\), and
\(\varepsilon^2>0\) if and only if \(\varepsilon\neq0\).
For any real \(\varepsilon,a,b\),
\[
 H_\varepsilon(a,b)^{\mathsf T}=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 ((\varepsilon=0\ \text{or}\ a=0)\ \text{and}\ b=0).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The energy difference is \((1-\varepsilon^2)b^2\geq0\). A real square
is positive precisely when its base is nonzero. The kernel equations are
\(\varepsilon^2a=0\) and \(b=0\), giving the stated alternative.
\end{proof}

The Lean declarations
\texttt{LaboratoryContracts.hodge\_family\_certificate} and
\texttt{hodge\_family\_kernel} use the existing matrix and energy definitions.
They do not infer topology from pixel brightness or an eigenvalue cutoff.
Exact zero changes the formal case even when a small positive value looks
indistinguishable on the canvas. The logarithmic mode remains available for
exploration; its generic values do not produce rational certificates.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic-order power determinants]
\label{prop:workbench-cover-certificate}
For \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&1\\1&5\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
and every \(k\in\N\), one has \(\det(J^k)=14^k>0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Direct evaluation gives \(\det J=15-1=14\). Determinant multiplicativity
gives the power identity, including \(k=0\), and positive numbers have positive
natural powers.
\end{proof}

The live interface also computes \(J^kx\bmod\Z^2\) exactly. The existing
covering-space argument explains its degree; the new Lean specialization
establishes the determinant components. Import respects the hundredth-step
point controls and powers \(0,\ldots,3\). An off-grid point is rejected
rather than rounded.

For a supplied nonnegative gain matrix \(G\), positive weights \(w_i\), and
proposed rate \(q\geq0\), the weighted laboratory checks
\[
 (Gw)_i\leq qw_i,\qquad s_i=qw_i-(Gw)_i,\qquad
 \max_i\frac{(Gw)_i}{w_i}\leq q.
\]
The earlier weighted-gain theorem gives a norm bound for this envelope.
The additional condition \(q<1\) gives its contraction flag. A holding
bound with \(q\geq1\) lacks that flag. A failed row remains a valid negative
calculation; its generated Lean file proves the exhibited violation. Neither
outcome establishes that a learned operator, discretization or quantum
feedback rule is dominated by the entered matrix.

\section{A probability space for the complete finite search}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-family false-positive bound]
\label{prop:workbench-familywise-bound}
Let \((\Omega,\mathcal A,\mu)\) be a probability space, \(I\) a finite set,
and \(A_j\in\mathcal A\). If \(\mu(A_j)\leq b_j\), then
\[
 \mu\Bigl(\bigcup_{j\in I}A_j\Bigr)
 \leq \min\Bigl(1,\sum_{j\in I}b_j\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The union is measurable. Finite subadditivity gives the sum bound, and the
probability-space assumption bounds its measure by one.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Even moments control a fixed-threshold scan]
\label{thm:workbench-even-moment-bound}
Let \(Z_j:\Omega\to\R\) be measurable for \(j\in I\), let \(k\in\N\),
and let \(t>0\) be fixed. Suppose \(|Z_j|^{2k}\) is integrable and
\(\int |Z_j|^{2k}\,d\mu\leq M_j\). Then
\[
 \mu\{\exists j\in I:Z_j\geq t\}
 \leq \min\left(1,\frac{\sum_{j\in I}M_j}{t^{2k}}\right).
\]
If every \(M_j\leq M\), the upper bound is at most
\(\min(1,|I|M/t^{2k})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The crossing event for score \(j\) implies
\(|Z_j|^{2k}\geq t^{2k}\). Markov's integral inequality gives
\[
 \mu\{Z_j\geq t\}\leq
 \frac{\int |Z_j|^{2k}\,d\mu}{t^{2k}}\leq\frac{M_j}{t^{2k}}.
\]
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:workbench-familywise-bound} and factor out the
common positive denominator. The uniform bound follows by summation.
\end{proof}

The Lean proof uses the Bochner integral, the probability-measure typeclass,
and the pinned mathlib Markov lemma
\texttt{MeasureTheory.mul\_meas\_ge\_le\_integral\_of\_nonneg}
\citep{wbMathlibPinned}. Measurability, integrability and true moment
inequalities remain hypotheses in the printed type. No independence between
scores is required. The mathematical interface permits \(k=0\), which is
usually uninformative; the live domain uses \(1\leq k\leq16\).

\begin{corollary}[An exact scalar budget implies conditional risk control]
\label{cor:workbench-moment-threshold}
Under the uniform assumptions above, if
\(|I|M\leq\alpha t^{2k}\), then
\(\mu\{\exists j\in I:Z_j\geq t\}\leq\alpha\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Divide by the positive \(t^{2k}\) and apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:workbench-even-moment-bound}.
\end{proof}

The browser checks the scalar premise exactly; its emitted Lean instance
applies the actual measure theorem with the remaining assumptions visible.
If \(\alpha=1\), the capped bound may meet the budget while
\(|I|M>\alpha t^{2k}\). The exporter then uses the capped uniform theorem
and does not assert the false stronger premise.

\section{A finite Gaussian model and exact variance envelope}

The declared illustrative null model is
\[
 \xi\sim N(0,I_{96}),\qquad Y=L\xi,\qquad
 Z_j=w_j^{\mathsf T}Y,\quad j=1,\ldots,288.
\]
The stored lower-triangular \(L\) and filters \(w_j\) were selected numerically.
Every stored binary64 coefficient is then interpreted as its exact dyadic
rational value. This defines a concrete finite model. It does not identify
the rounded factor with an unrounded analytic covariance kernel, or give a
pseudorandom output an ideal Gaussian distribution.

Independence and unit variances of the coordinates give
\[
 v_j=\operatorname{Var}(Z_j)=\|L^{\mathsf T}w_j\|_2^2.
\]
The builder computes these finite sums exactly with dyadic integers. An
independent Python implementation recomputes every full-column dot product
with rational arithmetic, compares all 288 variances, and checks
\[
 v_j\leq v_*=\frac{1000000000001}{1000000000000}.
\]
This outward envelope is deliberately slightly larger than one. Nominal
numerical normalization does not imply the exact identity \(v_j=1\).

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian moments for the declared finite null]
\label{prop:workbench-gaussian-moment}
Under this ideal Gaussian model, for \(k\geq1\),
\[
 \mathbb E|Z_j|^{2k}=(2k-1)!!\,v_j^k
 \leq M_k:=(2k-1)!!\,v_*^k.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A linear combination of independent centered Gaussians is centered Gaussian:
multiplication of characteristic functions gives
\(\prod_\ell e^{-s^2a_\ell^2/2}=e^{-s^2\sum_\ell a_\ell^2/2}\).
For the standard normal density
\(\phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}\), polynomial moments are finite,
the integration-by-parts boundary term vanishes, and \(\phi'(x)=-x\phi(x)\)
gives
\[
 \int_{\R}x^{2k}\phi(x)\,dx
 =(2k-1)\int_{\R}x^{2k-2}\phi(x)\,dx.
\]
Iteration from the zeroth moment yields \((2k-1)!!\). Scale by
\(\sqrt{v_j}\), handle \(v_j=0\) directly, and use monotonicity of
nonnegative natural powers.
\end{proof}

This is a written argument and explicit model obligation. The current Lean
module formalizes the subsequent measure implication, rather than the full
Gaussian product-space construction and moment identity. For \(k=8\) and
\(t=6\), the 288-filter bound is approximately \(2.06934\times10^{-4}\).
The decimal renders an exact rational result. It is a conservative bound
under the declared null, not a probability that a cosmological
interpretation is true.

The threshold and statistic must be fixed for this theorem. Exploring
thresholds and moment orders is supported, but selecting a new threshold
after inspecting the same sky changes the statistical procedure and needs
its own error-control argument. Adding filters likewise changes the
declared family. The theorem covers the 288 specified scores.

\section{Finite calibration ranks have a separate meaning}

The stored calibration has \(N=4095\) maxima. If \(c\) are at least as
large as the observed statistic, its finite rank is
\(r_N(c)=(1+c)/(N+1)\). Exact replay interprets stored coefficients and
calibration maxima as exact binary64 constants, performs score arithmetic
in \(\Q\), and compares without a floating-point tolerance. The original
canvas readout rounds arithmetic; close ties can distinguish the two.

\begin{proposition}[Finite rank arithmetic]\label{prop:workbench-rank-arithmetic}
For \(N,c\in\N\) with \(c\leq N\),
\[
 0<r_N(c),\qquad \frac1{N+1}\leq r_N(c)\leq1.
\]
For rational \(\alpha\),
\[
 r_N(c)\leq\alpha
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 1+c\leq\alpha(N+1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The denominator is positive and \(1\leq1+c\leq N+1\). Division gives
the bounds; multiplication by the positive denominator gives the equivalence.
\end{proof}

Both arithmetic components are Lean checked. They do not establish
exchangeability of simulated and observed scores, a probability law for the
fixed calibration sample, or a physical false-positive rate. The exact
resolution floor is \(1/4096\). At the four recorded signal-to-noise settings
\(0,1,3,5\), replay gives \(1893/4096,\ 741/4096,\ 7/2048,\ 1/4096\),
respectively. These are source-bound finite benchmark outputs.

\section{Connecting formal budgets, control and numerical residuals}

The same separation of obligations links the workbench to nonlinear operators
and quantum--tropical constructions. A nonnegative matrix is a gain envelope
only after bounding the actual model increments on a specified domain. A
rational certificate then checks the remaining finite inequality. A
probability certificate checks a risk budget after establishing the score
moments. A finite-resolution diagnostic supplies a consistency residual only
for its declared mesh family and observable. These interfaces compose when
their domains, norms and meanings agree.

For example, suppose a model update is contractive with factor \(q<1\) and
the numerical realization adds a disturbance bounded by \(\eta_n\). If
\(e_{n+1}\leq qe_n+\eta_n\), iteration gives
\[
 e_n\leq q^n e_0+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}q^{n-1-j}\eta_j.
\]
The earlier control chapter proves the relevant recurrence.
The finite-resolution chapter identifies spatial, temporal and observable
residuals that can contribute to \(\eta_j\), with real-arithmetic scope and
floating-point qualifications. A displayed \(q<1\) cannot supply a missing
residual estimate or prove that a substrate obeys the chosen equation.

For a generatively proposed substrate, identify measured states and
observables, fit a model with explicit uncertainty, establish its invariant
domain and gain envelope, quantify discretization and realization errors,
and then apply the finite certificate. Natural substrates require the same
identification work. Quantum channels retain complete positivity,
normalization, readout and noise obligations. Tropical weights describe a
declared dominant-scale or positive-gain calculation; omitted quantum phases
remain additional information.

The workbench makes exact local assertions actionable: expose a failed
premise, change a parameter, inspect its theorem type, and reproduce a checked
specialization. The continuum, statistical-model and hardware obligations
remain visible as the next mathematical or experimental work.
