## Assessment

The elementary conductor-count estimate and its application to the two original expansions are mathematically sound. They recover a known high-common-divisor sector in conductor coordinates. They do not establish new cancellation in the unresolved low-gcd sector or improve the accepted global analytic exponent. The original note calls the bound a new partial result; that novelty wording requires the correction described below. The author supplied explicitly qualified compact TeX proposals and NOVELTY_AND_SCOPE.md. Their final frozen revision is accepted for scope correction, with no new SC book theorem. The count/proof remains valid, while the older artifact and its manifest remain historical evidence.

The useful explanatory content is the distinction between generic four squarefree factors and the original internally coprime support, the explicit retention of canceled-prime zeros in each row direction, squarefree-pivot deletion bookkeeping, and the exact scale at which the attempted local completion returns the trivial length bound. These are algebraic clarification and a failure diagnosis, not a new analytic power gain.

## Original coefficient support and the pair count

In the accepted high window, a long-long assignment ab dividing an active squarefree m has Na,Nb>U and Nm<=2D, so the residual c=m/(ab) satisfies Nc<2D/U^2<=7. Since every good nonunit ideal has norm at least7, c is the unit ideal. Thus the original C_U(m) equals mu(m) times the number of ordered long factorizations ab=m, with all original coprimality and hyperbolic restrictions. This step is necessary before interpreting a product expansion as the original polynomial. It does not enlarge the signed support.

For two squarefree columns write h=(m,n), r=m/h, t=n/h. The three ideals are pairwise coprime and the representation is unique. Annular support gives Nr/Nt=Nm/Nn in[1/2,2]. If Nr Nt<=Y and V=max(Nr,Nt), then V^2<=2Y and Nh V<=2D. On the dyad V0<=V<2V0 the elementary ideal count gives O(V0^2) residual pairs and O(D/V0) choices for h. Nonempty dyads have V0<=2D, so no missing small-length additive1 changes this estimate. This gives O(DV0) pairs per dyad, whose geometric sum is O(Dsqrt(Y)). The diagonal is excluded before the argument; no nonprincipal-character bound is applied to it.

Dropping squarefreeness, coprimality, ray and factor restrictions only enlarges this positive count. For any delta>0, 2^omega(m)<=C_delta(Nm)^delta, by treating the finite set of primes with Np<2^(1/delta) separately. The actual factor multiplicities in CC and Cmu therefore cost D^epsilon. The complete literal block kernel has absolute value at most its row count O(R0), since every original raw symbol has modulus at most1. Multiplication by the common profile bound and D^-1 proves the stated O(||W||_infinity^2 D^epsilon R0 sqrt(Y)) bound for each exact sector. No sign cancellation, arbitrary-weight domination, or coefficient-diagonal energy estimate is used.

## Novelty comparison with the already completed high-gcd result

The identity Nq=Nm Nn/(Nh)^2 implies that Nq<=Y forces Nh>=D/sqrt(Y). Conversely Nh>=2D/sqrt(Y) forces Nq<=Y. The sectors are equivalent up to fixed annular constants. The existing research/short-row-high-gcd.md and planning/reviews/book/short-row-formal.md already prove the fixed-radial smooth high-gcd estimate O(D^(2+epsilon)/G^2). Substituting G=D/sqrt(Y) gives O(D^epsilon Y). Bounded divisor-factor multiplicities are absorbed into epsilon by the same pointwise divisor estimate.

At Y=P^2 on the full face and H=D^(3/4), P<=D^(1/4), hence Nh>=D/P>=D^(3/4). This is a strict subrange of the previously controlled smooth high-gcd region Nh>=D^(5/8). On that common smooth scope the older bound is O(D^epsilon P^2), stronger than the new elementary R0P bound because R0>=P. This is not a newly reached conductor region.

There is a real hypothesis distinction: the present elementary count applies directly to exact sharp literal six-factor blocks, with original masks and factor multiplicities, whereas the old Poisson estimate has a fixed smooth row profile. A nonpositive off-diagonal sector cannot simply be dominated by a smooth full positive energy. Therefore this audit does not claim the older smooth theorem literally implies every sharp block sector inequality. It classifies the current result as recovery/bookkeeping in an already identified easy high-gcd region, not new progress on the hard analytic frontier. The full A3/slack description was also already accepted in papers/covariance-budget-details.tex.

## Full face and neighboring losses

Put C=H^(1/3). On A1=A2=T, A3=C/T, A4=A5=A6=1 with1<=T<=C, R0=CT and P=max(T,C/T). Hence R0P=max(CT^2,C^2)<=C^3=H. This retains the whole A3 direction, including both endpoints, rather than only A3=1.

For a general block with Aj>=1, R0 Aj<=product Ai^i<=H for every j>=2. For j=1, R0 A1<=H(A1/A2). The accepted slack identity, including capacity slack and A4/A5/A6 terms, gives A1/A2<=D^(3eta) on the branch A1>=A2 whenever R0/max(A1,A2)>=H^(1/3)D^(-eta). The other branch has A1/A2<=1. Consequently R0P<=H D^(3eta), including neighboring blocks with nonunit A4,A5,A6. This is valid algebraic use of the prior face, not an extension to the remaining large-conductor sum.

## Raw local conductors and all zero masks

For arbitrary individually squarefree a,b,c,d the local exponent alpha+beta-gamma-delta may be0,+/-1,+/-2. Exponents+/-2 are nontrivial cubic local characters at every good prime, so a parity symmetric difference of four supports is wrong. The pattern(1,1,1,0) also shows that taking the symmetric difference of the two nonsquarefree product supports is wrong. On the original internally coprime support, each product has membership at most1, so only0,+/-1 occur. The accepted symmetric-difference conductor of the two squarefree products is therefore correct on its stated support.

In pivot direction j the exponent is j e mod6. On the original unequal-product support, every prime of q remains nontrivial for j=1,...,5, with sextic/cubic/quadratic local orders as appropriate; in particular A3 remains genuinely nonprincipal. For the relaxed +/-2 channel, j=3 becomes primitive-principal but retains its zero at the prime. Direction6 never supplies this oscillation and still retains all union zeros. The inequality P>=Aj in the long-conductor discussion must therefore be read with j in{1,...,5}, not j=6. The author was asked to make that explicit in the proposals.

The complete finite-field sums are exactly Gauss sums for nonzero exponent, and zero-extended principal Ramanujan sums for exponent0. CRT therefore gives sqrt(NQ_j) multiplied by the explicit Ramanujan factors at Z_j. A common prime of norm13 at its zero local frequency contributes12, so even the squared full transform can be144 times the square-root-conductor prediction. The zero frequency of a nonprincipal primitive component vanishes, but that fact does not control the incomplete original row sum.

Möbius deletion over every divisor of Z_j is exact and preserves the scaled profile, fixed ray restrictions and original zeros. The joint squarefree identity is also exact: the inner deletion sum vanishes if r meets E, while if r is coprime to E the usual square-divisor identity remains. The restriction(h,E)=1 makes d and h coprime, so the scaled argument is exactly d h^2 z. Multiplicativity gives the displayed theta(d)theta(h)^2 factors; it never restores a raw zero. Bounding the lifted inner sums by length and summing reciprocal norm squares of h gives only Aj times divisor losses.

## Completion boundary and the surviving hard target

The smooth unmasked Poisson trial is sound with fixed smooth seminorms and a genuinely nonprincipal primitive conductor. Its zero mode vanishes and planar frequency counting gives min(Aj,sqrt(Nq)). That derivative constant is not uniform under approximation of the original sharp row boundary, so this trial is not asserted for the original sharp kernel. The unconditional sharp bound used in the count is only O(R0).

On Nq>P^2 every nonprincipal pivot j<=5 has Aj<=P<sqrt(Nq). Thus even the favorable trial takes its trivial length term, including after every divisor/square-divisor rescaling. At h=1, Nq=Nm Nn is in[D^2,4D^2]. On the full H=D^(3/4) face every such pivot has Aj<=D^(1/4), so sextic, cubic and quadratic completion all remain far inside this short regime. Changing local order does not shorten the conductor. The proposed per-conductor triangle loses the signed column/frequency interaction and gives the inherited inadequate absolute bound D R0, not a new BT_sigma estimate. The actual low-gcd, especially h=1, signed fourth and mixed moments remain the next problem.
