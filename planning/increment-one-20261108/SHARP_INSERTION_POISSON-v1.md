## The sharp interval moment

For the exact pure scales \(A\asymp R\asymp D^{3/4}\), put \(L_{\max}=D^{4/5}\), \(\Lambda=1\). For the exact mixed scales \(A\asymp D^{5/12},R\asymp D^{7/12}\), put \(L_{\max}=D^{5/8}\), \(\Lambda=D^{1/20}\). Allow fixed multiples in these length caps. For \(1\le L\lesssim L_{\max}\), an unweighted ideal character sum over any interval in \([L,2L]\), with either endpoint convention, satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{RMS}_{u}\left(\sum_{Nm\text{ in interval}}\psi_u(m)\right)
 \ll_\epsilon (DH)^\epsilon\sqrt{L\Lambda}.                         \tag{1}
\]
It holds for the conjugate character as well. We prove this for differences of disks of norm bound comparable to \(L\). Empty and bounded subunit lengths have the evident unit-count bound. The auxiliary ideal character is the exact primitive \(\psi_u\), with all its zeros, even on ideals whose support meets \(S\).

Let \(F\) be the planar disk indicator of radius \(\sqrt{\lambda}\) with \(\lambda\asymp L\), convolved with a fixed nonnegative radial \(C^\infty_c\) bump of unit mass rescaled to radius \(\delta\sqrt{\lambda}\), \(0<\delta\le1/10\). The difference from the exact disk weight is bounded by 1 and is supported in a norm interval of width \(O(\delta L)\). This includes any integer boundary atom regardless of which convention is used. The boundary norm is at most
\[
 (DH)^\epsilon\sqrt{\Phi(A,L)/A}\left(\sqrt{\delta L}+L^{1/6}\right). \tag{2}
\]
Indeed decompose each ideal in the shell uniquely as \(\mathfrak s\,m_0 v^6\) with \(\mathfrak s\) supported at \(S\) and \(m_0\) good sixth-free. At fixed \(\mathfrak s,v\), \(m_0\) has length \(O(L/M)\) where \(M=N\mathfrak s (Nv)^6\), and its norm lies in a shell of width \(O(\delta L/M)\). Each integer norm supports at most divisor-many ideals. The sixth-free sieve for coefficients with squared mass \(O((DH)^\epsilon(1+\delta L/M))\) gives the RMS bound with \(\sqrt{\Phi(A,L)/A}\), by monotonicity. All fixed raw factors have modulus at most one. Sum the square roots by Minkowski, using the convergent \(\sum_{\mathfrak s,v}M^{-1/2}\) for the first term and \(O(L^{1/6}\log^C(2L))\) for the second. In the second count \(M\ll L\) is necessary, and \(\mathfrak s\) has support in the fixed \(S\). Throughout the two stated ranges \(\Phi(A,L)/A\ll L^{2/3}\), as \(L\ll A^3\). Thus the second term of (2) is \(O((DH)^\epsilon\sqrt L)\). This step uses the sixth-free coefficient estimate only on the residual support, rather than treating an arbitrary thin shell as a sixth-free family.

Apply the exact primitive radial Poisson formula to \(F\), as in DUAL_CUTOFF_PRODUCT-v1. Its normalization is \(2\gamma_u/(\sqrt3\sqrt{Q_u})\) times the ideal sum of the Fourier transform of \(F\) with the conjugate primitive values at the dual labels. The dual argument has squared radius \(4Nk/(3Q_u)\). The dual zero is absent. This formula applies to the radial convolution by the same residue-class Poisson derivation, as its weight is smooth and compactly supported. In a dyad \(T\le Nk<2T\), put \(Y=\sqrt{LT/R}\). The Fourier transform and its first log derivative in the argument, including a fixed-width log cutoff on the actual \(Nk/Q_u\) interval, are bounded respectively by
\[
 C_M L(1+Y)^{-3/2}(1+\delta Y)^{-M},\qquad
 C_M L(1+Y)^{-1/2}(1+\delta Y)^{-M}.
\]
For the disk itself, the scaled transform is \(L\) times the one-dimensional Fourier transform of \(2\sqrt{1-v^2}\) on \([-1,1]\). Its first derivative uses the same integrand multiplied by \(-2\pi i v\). Both transforms at frequency \(Y\) are \(O((1+Y)^{-3/2})\). Integrate by parts once, split the derivative integral within \(1/Y\) of the two endpoints, and integrate by parts again on the remaining interval. The endpoint contribution to the derivative integral is \(O(Y^{-1/2})\), and the boundary values and second-derivative integral in the remaining region cost \(O(Y^{1/2})\) before the second division by \(Y\). A log derivative costs at most one extra \(1+Y\). The bump transform and its log derivative give arbitrary powers of \((1+\delta Y)^{-1}\) by smooth compact support. These arguments also bound the small \(Y\) region.

For any such log cutoff \(m\), split its Fourier integral at a frequency \(1+\|m'\|_2/\|m\|_2\), using Cauchy and Plancherel on either side. This gives
\(\|\widehat m\|_1\ll \|m\|_2^{1/2}(\|m\|_2+\|m'\|_2)^{1/2}\), with zero interpreted separately. The preceding log bounds therefore give Fourier separation with absolute mass
\[
 O_M\bigl(L(1+Y)^{-1}(1+\delta Y)^{-M}\bigr).
\]
In particular the exact phases in \(Nk,Q_u\) separate, with the actual \(Q_u^{-1/2}\gamma_u\) still a row multiplier. The all-ideal dual moment at \(T\) is bounded by \((DH)^\epsilon\sqrt{T\Phi(A,T)/A}\), allowing a \(T^\epsilon\) subpower for the unbounded tail. The product with the radial mass and \(Q_u^{-1/2}\) is bounded by
\[
 (DH)^\epsilon\sqrt L\,\sqrt{\Phi(A,T)/A}
       \frac{Y}{1+Y}(1+\delta Y)^{-M}.
\]
Set \(T_0=\max(1,R/(L\delta^2))\). The dyads up to \(T_0\) cost at most a logarithm times \(\sqrt{\Phi(A,T_0)/A}\). Above it use degree at most one of the sieve polynomial and choose \(M\) fixed sufficiently large. The polynomial \(T_0\) absorbs the subpower of its length, and the remaining arbitrarily large tail sums geometrically. We conclude that the smoothed disk moment is
\[
 \ll_\epsilon (DH)^\epsilon\sqrt L\sqrt{\Phi(A,T_0)/A}.            \tag{3}
\]

For pure, use the direct bounded-coefficient estimate for \(L\lesssim D^{3/8}\). For larger \(L\) choose \(\delta=c A/\Phi(A,L)\) with a fixed small \(c>0\). Write \(l=\log_D L\). The exponent of \(\Phi(A,L)/A\) is
\(\Delta(l)=\max(0,l-a,l/3-a/6,5l/6-2a/3)\) with \(a=3/4\). The exponent of \(T_0\) above one is bounded by \(3/4-l+2\Delta(l)\). This convex function is at most \(3/8\) on \([3/8,4/5]\) by checking endpoints. Thus both (2) and (3) cost \(O((DH)^\epsilon\sqrt L)\). For mixed, use the direct bound for \(l\le43/120\), where \(\Phi(A,L)/A\ll D^{1/20}\). Otherwise use \(\delta=c D^{1/20}A/\Phi(A,L)\), reducing it to \(1/10\) if necessary. Now \(a=5/12\), and the exponent \(7/12-l+2(\Delta(l)-1/20)\) is at most \(43/120\) on \([43/120,5/8]\) by convexity and the two endpoint values. Fixed multiplicative constants only enlarge \(T_0\) and the comparisons by constants. This proves (1) with \(\Lambda=D^{1/20}\) as stated.

The same bound holds when the summed ideals are squarefree and avoid a fixed finite prime union of polynomial norm. Let \(F_0\) be the squarefree ideal of this forbidden union, including any original \(S\)-deletions. Expand squarefreeness by \(\sum_{s^2\mid m}\mu(s)\), restricted to \((s,F_0)=1\), and the fixed deletion by \(\sum_{e\mid(m,F_0)}\mu(e)\). These restrictions give the same product on all ideals, because the deletion already kills the forbidden terms. Set \(m=e s^2 n\), keeping the fixed raw factors. The new length is \(O(L/(Ne(Ns)^2))\). Apply (1) after the substitution, with subunit endpoints counted directly if nonempty. The \(s\)-sum with \(1/Ns\) is harmonic and the \(e\)-choices cost at most a divisor factor. This yields the squarefree deleted bound \(O((DH)^\epsilon\sqrt{L\Lambda})\). Finally a factor \((Nm/L)^{it}\) costs only \(O(1+|t|)\) by exact partial summation inside the interval, using its sharp subinterval estimates. There is no assertion of this moment with arbitrary additional column coefficients.

## Return to the original cross

Fix the original partner deletion allocation and \(J\)-dyad, as in TWO_FAMILY_CROSS-v1. First consider the entire rectangle with the equality subtraction deferred. The \(I\)-family before common-prime deletion consists of good squarefree ideals in its sharp norm interval, with an additional upper frequency clipping, and avoidance of fixed prime labels. In particular \(\beta+1\le5\) excludes only the fixed support of old residue five, once \((x,I)=1\) is imposed. The exact allowed \(I\)'s include any overlap with fixed \(g,v\) permitted by the original formula. Their raw multipliers still vanish when appropriate. The \(x\)-coefficient retains the full ordered cutoff and is divisor bounded apart from the actual \(1/Nx\). Expand \((x,I)=1\) by a common squarefree \(s\), writing \(x=sx'\) and \(I=sI'\). The squarefree shortened insertion \(I'\) avoids \(s\), and its exact remaining fixed masks are precisely deletions of the kind just estimated. The expansion remains exact after extending the rectangle to the invalid pairs. The independent deletion masks in each array and the raw factor \(\psi_u(s)\overline{\psi_u(s)}\) are retained. The original profile uses the physical norms, including \(Ns\).

Use the exact compact log separation of the partner radial weight. Its Fourier mass with the extra \(1+|t|\) is bounded by \(O(\mathcal M_{24}(W)(1+y'_1)^{-4})\), by three integrations by parts and the corresponding log derivatives of the smooth compact profile. For example four Euclidean Fourier derivatives and sufficiently many integrations by parts in the radial direction are covered by \(\mathcal M_{24}\). Use the sharp insertion estimate on the array at each \(t\), and the original physical sieve on the \(x'\) array. The first-pivot row Cauchy bound per \(s\) is now
\[
 \ll_\epsilon (DH)^\epsilon
       \frac{\sqrt{K/B}}{Ns}\sqrt{\Phi(A,B)\,A\Lambda},
\]
apart from the integrated profile. This follows from the squared \(x'\) mass \(O((DH)^\epsilon/(B Ns))\) and the insertion row norm \(O((DH)^\epsilon\sqrt{A K\Lambda/Ns})\). The harmonic \(s\)-sum and the sum over the frozen second pivot are the same as before. The exact primitive conductors and fixed phases remain in the row factors throughout.

The subtraction in the actual complement is the middle equal-norm piece. Bound that piece separately by its actual scalar count with the two half radial powers. For each \(x\), equality \(Nz_*Nx=Nk_*NI\) determines at most one integer \(NI\). There are divisor-many ideal labels of that norm, so counting \(I\) costs only a subpower at fixed \(x\), even on equal-norm fibres. The \(x\)-count with \(1/Nx\) is harmonic on its dyad. Count the original rows by \(O(R)\). Since \(B\asymp K\), the remaining scalar and label weights are exactly the half-power comparison of TWO_FAMILY_CROSS-v1 with no sieve factor, bounded by \(R/E\) up to the common costs. In this count keep the original middle window endpoints, and bound it only after imposing the equality, original coprimality and the raw weights. Its absolute harmonic \(d\)-sum costs \(O((DH)^\epsilon R)\). This is why no additive equality phase has to be inserted into the sharp Poisson moment.

Consequently (5) and (6) of TWO_FAMILY_CROSS-v1 on comparable dyads can replace \(\Phi(A,K)\) by \(A\Lambda\) in the entire rectangle bound, then add the separate \(R/E\) bound at fixed \(d\) for the actual middle subtraction. Keep \((1+K/R)^{-7/2}\) using the original partner primal test and the two half powers. The original conjugate \(q_\ell\) and \(d\)-weights remain valid, with the extra \(Nc\) charge disjoint in (6).

In pure, when \(B,K\lesssim D^{4/5}\), the half-power bound summed over \(d\) is \(O((DH)^\epsilon\mathcal M_{24}(W)^2D^{13/16})\), because the largest remaining sieve and decay factor is attained up to constants at \(B\asymp R\) and \(\Phi(A,R)\ll D^{7/8}\). The three-half-power bound at fixed \(d\) is \(O((DH)^\epsilon\mathcal M_{24}(W)^2 D^{11/16}/\sqrt{Nd})\) before adding the equality term. Summing \(Nd\le D^{1/8}\) gives the target \(D^{3/4+\epsilon}\). At larger comparable \(B,K\) use the prior half-power long bound, already at target beyond \(D^{51/64}\). Thus both conclusions hold for the full comparable increment-one sector. The all-\(d\) bound improves the old \(7/8\) comparison but still exceeds the target.

In mixed, for \(B,K\lesssim D^{5/8}\), the corresponding all-\(d\) power is \(57/80\), by \(A_2\asymp D^{1/6}\), \(\Phi(A,R)\ll D^{5/8}\), and \(A\Lambda\asymp D^{5/12+1/20}\). Again the sieve and retained decay factor is largest up to constants near \(B\asymp R\), since \(\Phi\) is nondecreasing and grows by at most degree one above \(R\). The equality count costs \(R\asymp D^{7/12}\). At larger comparable \(B,K\) the old half-power long comparison is at most \(D^{49/72}\) from its value at \(B=D^{5/8}\) and subsequent decay. Hence the \(57/80\) bound holds across the full comparable increment-one sector at mixed. These are exact-sector bounds. They do not settle the generic pure divisor tail, other increment mixtures, or the whole signed cross.
