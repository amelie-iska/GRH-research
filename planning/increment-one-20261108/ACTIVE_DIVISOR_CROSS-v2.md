 \frac{Nc\, E_0^{1/2-\theta}E_1^{\theta-1}

## Exact sign cancellation of the outer Möbius factor on a prime-divisor slice

Take \(h_0=h_1=1\), \(e_0=e_{1,i}=1\), \(\ell=1\), and equality labels \(z_{0,0}=k_0=g\) with \(g\) a fixed good prime ideal. On the partner take \(z=x=p_1p_2\), where \(p_1,p_2\) are distinct good prime ideals of norms comparable to \(D^{3/8}\), and take \(k=I\), with \(I\) good squarefree, \(NI\asymp Nx\asymp R\) and \(NI\ne Nx\). Take \(x,I,g,u\) pairwise coprime on the relevant raw support. Then \(z_*=1\) and the matched good frequency is \(x\). The defect is exactly cutoff erasure of \(x\) and fresh exponent-one increase on \(I\). There are no conjugate or \(d\)-residue changes. Both \(x\) factors fit individually in the partner cutoff and \(Nx>U\) for large \(D\).

For a nonempty example, specialize to the permitted pure row family with \(u_0=1\) and its primary section. Choose a degree-one prime ideal \(v_1\) in a fixed relative dyad with capacity slack. For the four factors of \(x,I\), use distinct degree-one prime ideals in sufficiently narrow fixed relative norm intervals near \(D^{3/8}\), with distinct rational primes and \(\mathfrak p\) outside their supports. The same pinned fixed-field prime-ideal theorem used below supplies these choices, as inert prime norms are squares and give only \(O(D^{3/16})\) labels up to a constant times \(D^{3/8}\). Then \(NI/Nx\) is close to one and is unequal to one. The sign identity holds for any retained row and labels satisfying the stated assumptions, regardless of this existence specialization.

Let \(d=\mathfrak p\) be a good prime ideal coprime to all these fixed labels with \(E=N\mathfrak p\) in a fixed interval of scale \(D/R\). Choose the fixed lower and upper constants of that interval so \(E Nx\le D\). The equality test has \(X'=D/E\asymp R>D^{3/8}\), \(Nk_0=Ng>1\), and \(M_0=R E J_g/D\asymp1\), where \(J_g\) is its actual dyadic cutoff scale. Thus the original upper middle test holds for sufficiently large \(D\) at fixed \(\eta>0\). Both primal tests hold. The partner is in the exact complement because \(NI\ne Nx\). Its original \(e_{1,1}=\mathfrak p\) term is primal-zero since \(D/(E^2 J_x)\ll D^{-1/4}\), with \(J_x\asymp Nx\). The selected equality term has \(e_{1,0}=1\); an equality allocation with \(e_{1,0}=\mathfrak p\) is a different term and is not included in this sign calculation.

On the equality side \(Ng\,Nd\le U\) for large \(D\), so every ordered \(d\)-assignment fits. The same calculation works for any squarefree \(d\) in this range. The sum over assigning each prime to neither cutoff or to one of the two cutoffs is \(1-1-1=-1\), independently at each prime. Consequently
\(\beta_{d,U,U^2}(g)=\mu(d)\beta_{1,U,U^2}(g)\) exactly. On the partner side \(x\) must split as \(p_1,p_2\) in one of the two orders, because \(Nx>U\). For the selected prime \(d\), assigning \(\mathfrak p\) to either cutoff is impossible, since \(E Np_i>U\) for both \(i\). Hence \(\beta_{d,U,U^2}(x)=\beta_{1,U,U^2}(x)\). In particular the exact product in the cross is
\[
 \mu(d)\,\beta_{d,U,U^2}(g)\,\overline{\beta_{d,U,U^2}(x)}
   =\beta_{1,U,U^2}(g)\,\overline{\beta_{1,U,U^2}(x)}.
\]
This is an identity for the full ordered cutoff coefficients on this slice, including all their permitted assignments. The right side is a nonzero constant independent of \(d\). The remaining row phase is also independent of \(d\) at the fixed labels. Thus the outer Möbius sign supplies no sign cancellation on this selected prime-divisor slice.

For example with nonzero real \(W\), the radial transform \(G_W\) is real on positive arguments and analytic there, and is not identically zero by injectivity of the Fourier transform. One can take a sufficiently small fixed relative interval of \(E\) at the scale \(D/R\) on which \(G_W(D/(E Q_u))\) is bounded away from zero, and take \(NI/Nx\) sufficiently close to one but unequal to one. The two radial factors then have product of fixed nonzero sign throughout that interval. This choice is compatible with the primal upper bound on \(E\), since the initial scale constant may be chosen small. If counting primes in that fixed relative interval, the pinned fixed-field input in rough-mask-details.tex, Thorner--Zaman, DOI \(10.2140/\mathrm{ant}.2019.13.1039\), gives order \((D/R)/\log(2D/R)\). Removing the primes in the fixed labels' polynomial-size ideal support removes only \(O(1)\) such large primes. This uses no prime theorem uniform in a varying conductor. After factoring out the fixed row phase and cutoff coefficients, the selected \(d\)-scalar with its \(E^{-2}\) weight is thus of order \((D/R)^{-1}/\log(2D/R)\) in this slice. The example diagnoses only the failure of a uniform fixed power saving from the outer \(\mu(d)\) on this scalar family. It gives no lower bound for the sum over the other allocations, row phases, or the full signed cross.
