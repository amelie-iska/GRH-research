# Exact eligibility in the two ordered cutoff factors

This note governs the coupled comparison with COUPLED_POWERFUL_CROSS-v2, including its \(P=1\) case. The earlier increment-one draft is preserved as research history.

Keep the two actual cutoff factors squarefree before imposing their mutual coprimality. At fixed allocation of the cutoff base and the \(s_1\)-primes, the two residual factors individually avoid the primes already allocated to that factor; both avoid \(s_1\) after the exact partition. When using the squarefree common divisor \(w\), require it to satisfy the original fixed prime eligibility in both residual arrays and impose individual avoidance of \(w\) on each further residual factor. Thus at an admissible pre-projector pair \(a,b\), the signed lifts are exactly \(w\mid(a,b)\), each counted once, with multiplier \(\mu(w)\psi_u(w)^2\). Every excluded local valuation is still excluded after stripping, and the projector equals \(1_{(a,b)=1}\) times the exact original raw phase. The fixed factors extracted when imposing one-sided rational content carry their own raw values and individual deletions in exactly the same way. No coprimality condition with the auxiliary Poisson frequency is inserted.

The separate insertion projector similarly tests \(s_1\) itself against the full fixed forbidden union before stripping it. A residual deletion alone can miss a forbidden prime already stripped, and the failed first control is retained as evidence of this exact mistake. The corrected controls test the primitive phases, common masks and signed grouped coefficients before the positive row bounds.

For the powerful tail the \(P\)-dyad and the \(r'\)-norm threshold are retained inside the grouped coefficients. Freezing all of \(P\) in the tail would only recover a weaker interpolating estimate and does not justify the uniform \(1/17\) loss. The successful step keeps \(P'\) and \(e_P\) grouped with \((r')^2m\), after extracting only \(P_s\). The \(\mu(d)\) sign is used in the exact expression and then bounded in absolute value. Neither the scale comparison nor the sparse tail proves an estimate on that remaining signed divisor average itself.
