# Signed two-cell estimate for a smooth full intermediate-divisor tail

7 November 2026 UTC. Author checked; independent review pending. This is a refinement of COHERENT_ANNULAR_TAIL-v1. It keeps the actual annular coefficients and original unweighted two-pivot rows, and changes the selection from a sharp divisor threshold to a fixed smooth one. The argument applies two signed Mertens estimates on disjoint cells before taking a row norm. Acceptance requires the literal endpoint and mask identities, both cell prefixes, their mutual coprimality, and all joint deletion budgets.

## 1. Claim and scope

Keep the setting of COHERENT_ANNULAR_TAIL-v1 (1)–(3): good ideals in the Eisenstein field, fixed \(S\supseteq\{\mathfrak p:\mathfrak p\mid 6\}\), fixed \(\nu\), the one common complex \(W\in C_c^\infty\) supported in \([1,2]\), \(U\ge7\) and \(U^2\le D\le7U^2/2\). The actual unweighted two-pivot rows have literal fixed \(S\)-supported part and outside-\(S\) physical ideal \(v_1v_2^2\) of norm at most \(H_b\le H\), with the original raw zeros, ray, coprimality and capacity restrictions. Use the inherited physical sixth-free norm (phy:input/phy:short) only for this family and its frozen-factor comparisons. Let \(\psi\) be a fixed smooth function on \((0,\infty)\), zero on \((0,1]\), one on \([2,\infty)\), and \(0\le\psi\le1\). Let \(\mathscr T_\psi^{BC}(Y)\) be (1) of the preceding note in bilinear form, with \(\mathbf1_{Nd\ge Y}\) replaced by \(\psi(Nd/Y)\); \(B,C\) are the two coefficient types. The sum still uses every good squarefree intermediate divisor, and its complement uses exactly \(1-\psi(Nd/Y)\).

Assume \(M_S(x)=O_{S,\sigma}(x^\sigma)\) for fixed \(2/3<\sigma\le1\). Let \(M_4\) denote the maximum of the sup norms of the first four log derivatives and the function itself. For \(1\le Y\le2D\), for the actual \(A_U\) on both sides,
\[
 |\mathscr T_\psi^{AA}(Y)|
 \ll (DH)^\epsilon M_4(\psi)M_4(W)^2
 \left[
 Y^{-(1-\sigma)/2}
 \bigl(H_b+H_b^{5/6}(D/Y)^{1/3}+H_b^{1/3}(D/Y)^{5/6}\bigr)+D/Y
 \right]. \tag{1}
\]
Constants may depend on the fixed field, \(S,\nu,\sigma,\epsilon\), and the inherited sieve and fixed Mertens bound. The formula is zero if the original profile or the row family is zero. This is a sufficient upper bound, with no lower bound claimed. The \(M_S\) power \(7/8+\eta\) is conditional on exactly the pinned fixed-field input of COHERENT_J_TAIL-v1 §4, with the same public-source caveat. It is not a varying-conductor estimate or a new zero-free theorem.

## 2. Literal disjoint cells and separate strict cutoffs

On the active annulus the original coefficient is \(A_U=G-\mu\), with \(G=\mu\tau_{>U}\) and \(\tau_{>U}\) the number of ordered two-factor splittings with both norms strictly above \(U\), by the preceding inclusion–exclusion identity. Expand into the four pairings. For \(G\)–\(G\), retain the ordered factor labels \(a=a_1a_2,\ b=b_1b_2\) in the full Möbius inversion. The full intermediate divisor is allocated into the four disjoint cells \(d_{ij}\); the residuals are squarefree and disjoint within each side and may overlap across the two sides. Their exact weight is \(\mu(a)\mu(b)\mu(d)\nu(a)\overline{\nu(b)}\chi_a(u)\overline{\chi_b(u)}\), subject to all original masks; cancellation of the two \(d\)-phases uses \((d,u)=1\) and \(d\) good.

Partition the cell norms into half-open dyads \([X_{ij},2X_{ij})\), \(X_{ij}\) powers of two starting at 1, and write \(F=\prod X_{ij}\). Of the two pairs \(\{11,22\}\) and \(\{12,21\}\), select the one with larger scale product, breaking ties by a fixed order of pairs. Denote its moving cells \(c_1,c_2\), with scales \(X_1,X_2\), \(Z=X_1X_2\ge F^{1/2}\). Freeze the other two ideals, with product \(d_0\), norm \(F_0\). For a mixed pairing select the largest of its two cells, with \(Z=X_1\ge F^{1/2}\); for \(\mu\)–\(\mu\) take its only cell, with \(Z=F\). These latter cases have one moving cell. Write \(r=1,2\) for the number moving, \(L=D/(F_0Z)\), and \(v_i=Nc_i/X_i\in[1,2)\). Nonempty dyads have \(Y/2^4<F<2D\), \(L\ge1/2\), and \(Na,Nb\in[L/2^r,2L]\). The left strict inequality for \(F\) follows from support of \(\psi\); the right follows from the annulus.

Each moving cell in a selected matching is the only moving cell in its row and its column. In a gap factor on side \(a\) the moving threshold is \(Nc_i>U/(C_{ai}Na_{j_i})\), with \(C_{ai}\) the norm of the frozen part of the cell product in that factor. The corresponding side \(b\) threshold uses \(C_{bi}Nb_{k_i}\). A gap factor not containing a moving cell retains its own strict frozen inequality. For a cell with two thresholds take their maximum; split into two order branches by comparing the two positive integers \(C_{ai}Na_{j_i}\), \(C_{bi}Nb_{k_i}\), assigning equality once. Both integers are bounded by \(2D\) on the displayed residual support, since each \(C\le F_0\) and \(Na,Nb\le2L\). To separate each comparison use the Fourier expansion of a cyclic step: take a cyclic group with the smallest even order \(M>8D+4\), extend the positive (or nonnegative) half of the difference range by an interval of residues. Its Fourier coefficient at index \(j\ne0\) has magnitude at most \(C/\min(j,M-j)\) by the geometric sum; the zero coefficient is at most one. Thus the sum of absolute coefficients is \(O(\log(2D))\), and the phases separate between sides. Two comparisons cost at most its square. In each branch every moving cell's prefix now depends on labels of just one side (or has no gap threshold). All norms tied at a threshold retain the stated strict inequality. Distinct ideals with equal norm are not identified.

## 3. Two signed prefixes, with their overlap removed exactly

For two moving cells expand \(\mathbf1_{(c_1,c_2)=1}\) by the common ideal \(\ell\) with sign \(\mu(\ell)\). Put \(E=N\ell\), \(c_i=\ell x_i\); then \(E<2\min(X_1,X_2)\). The squarefree constraints on \(c_i\) become squarefree \(x_i\) avoiding \(\ell\); the product of the two cell signs becomes \(\mu(x_1)\mu(x_2)\). Keep the masks \((\ell,S d_0abu)=1\) and put \(S_0=S\cup\operatorname{supp}(d_0\ell)\). The two \(x_i\) must both avoid \(S_0abu\), with no remaining coprimality imposed between them in a fixed Möbius term. For one moving cell take \(\ell=(1)\) with no summation and \(E=1\). Frozen cells remain pairwise coprime and avoid \(Sabu\).

For each cell independently apply the exact deletion identity for \(M_{S_0\cup\operatorname{supp}(abu)}\): convolve \(\mu\) restricted outside \(S_0\) with the indicator of all powers supported on \(\operatorname{supp}(abu)\setminus S_0\). For an outside prime in the deletion power, expand its presence in the union by the four signed placements: row \(u\); or, if absent from \(u\), column \(a\), column \(b\), minus both. All powers of that prime in a tuple have the same placement. Denote each resulting tuple's ideal by \(h_i\). Its mass imposes \(Nh_i\le2X_i/E\) or the prefix vanishes. On live joint tuples a prime assigned to the row in one tuple cannot appear in a column placement in the other, because the column placement requires the row to avoid it. Let \(r_u,r_a,r_b\) be the radicals of the corresponding **unions across tuples**. If the same prime occurs in both tuples it must not be counted twice in any row or column norm reduction. This restriction leaves the original union masks exactly; the fixed \(\ell,d_0\) masks are not restored by using primitive characters.

On the Mellin line with real part zero, write each profile by Fourier inversion in log coordinate. On all \(v_i\in[1,2)\) write the last factor \(\psi(F_0Z\prod v_i/Y)\) by the same inversion, multiplying by a fixed \(C_c^\infty\) bump in \(\log(\prod v_i)\) equal to 1 on \([0,\log4]\). The absolute integrals of each Fourier transform with weight \((1+|t|)^2\) are bounded by \(C M_4\) of the corresponding function (four integrations by parts for \(|t|\ge1\)). The constant is uniform in \(F_0 Z/Y\), since translation in the log variable preserves the derivative sup norms of \(\psi\) and the bump has fixed compact support. Profile phases in \(Na,Nb,F_0Z/D\) separate and have modulus one. Each moving cell has the same total frequency \(\tau\) up to the common sign convention, a signed sum of the three frequencies. After deletion the ideal left in its prefix has norm variable \(n\), with \(Nc_i=E Nh_i n\); the modulation is \((E Nh_i n/X_i)^{i\tau}\).

By the finite Euler product, \(M_{S_0}(t)\ll (EF_0)^\epsilon t^\sigma\) for \(t\ge1\) and it vanishes below one. For each cell the actual range of \(n\) is the dyad rescaled by \(E Nh_i\), intersected with the single strict lower cutoff selected by the branch, if present. Ordinary partial summation on this interval bounds its signed modulated sum by
\[
 C(EF_0)^\epsilon (X_i/(E Nh_i))^\sigma(1+|\tau|).
\]
In particular, for the open lower threshold subtract the prefix including equality, whereas for the dyadic inclusive left endpoint subtract the prefix strictly below it; the upper dyadic endpoint always uses a strict prefix. The variation of the modulation on any such interval is at most \(|\tau|\log 2\). A nonempty interval has upper bound \(2X_i/(E Nh_i)>1\), or has no term, so the estimate applies also to subunit lower cutoffs. The exact value belongs to the chosen side of the comparison, including its own norm labels and all endpoints. Crucially, take the product of the signed prefixes before applying a row norm. The factor \((1+|\tau|)^r\) is integrable against the three transform bounds above since \(r\le2\) and \(1+|\tau|\le\prod_{j=1}^3(1+|t_j|)\).

## 4. Physical norm and the joint deletion cost

At each separated term use all literal residual labels to form column coefficient vectors on \(a,b\). The multiplicity per ideal is divisor bounded; the unitary profile/comparison phases cost nothing. Strip off the uniform prefix bounds and integrate after the row estimate. The two squared coefficient masses are bounded, up to subpowers, by \(L/Nr_a\) and \(L/Nr_b\): they are supported on multiples in the annulus for the residual, and empty if the respective radical norm exceeds \(2L\). For the operator use \(CL\ge1\) as in the preceding note; subunit coefficient lengths are counted directly. Freeze each row valuation on \(r_u\), costing \(2^{\omega(r_u)}\), and restrict the remaining physical length by \(H_b/Nr_u\). For each fixed valuation the removed raw character is a separate column multiplier (possibly zero); discard remaining row restrictions only in the two positive operator norms, after keeping them in the identity. Cauchy in the actual rows then gives the following unnormalized bound per tuple, with the Mellin/Fourier and subpower factors suppressed:
\[
 \frac{Z^\sigma L}{E^{r\sigma}(\prod_i Nh_i)^\sigma
                           \sqrt{Nr_a Nr_b}}\,
 2^{\omega(r_u)}\Phi_6(H_b/Nr_u,CL),\qquad
 \Phi_6(H',L')=H'+L'+(H')^{5/6}(L')^{1/3}+(H')^{1/3}(L')^{5/6}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The outside sign \(\mu(\ell)\mu(d_0)\) remains until taking the triangle at this step; no sign or independence is assumed for the cells or residues. The subpower factors for \(F_0,E\) are bounded by \((DH)^\epsilon\) after reducing their individual exponents; \(F_0 E\le C D\) on support.

For the three monomials with a row factor, sum all the deletion powers. A prime appearing in exactly one tuple gets exponent at least \(\sigma+1/3\) if placed on the row, or \(\sigma+1/2\) if on a column. If it appears in both, the base exponent is at least \(2\sigma\) before any radical gain. Higher powers are geometric; the placement and valuation multiplicities at each prime are bounded constants. Each of these local sums therefore differs from 1 by \(O((Np)^{-1-\delta})\) with some fixed \(\delta>0\) because \(\sigma>2/3\). Thus the joint sum is bounded, with no artificial independent radical gains. The subsequent \(\ell\) sum for \(r=2\) converges using \(2\sigma>1\) and elementary ideal counting; for \(r=1\) it is absent.

For the \(L'\) monomial the column powers still sum with a convergent product by the same singly placed/repeated analysis. The row powers \(h_{u,i}\) have no radical gain, so retain \(Nh_{u,i}\le2X_i/E\) separately. Bound \(2^{\omega(r_u)}\) by a subpower, and apply ideal counting to each truncated positive sum; their combined cost is at most
\[
 C_\epsilon (Z/E^r)^{1-\sigma+\epsilon},
\]
with the logarithm at \(\sigma=1\) included in the subpower. Here each \(2X_i/E>1\) on live terms, so subunit cutoff constants are harmless. This may relax disjointness for the *upper* positive row and column placement sums, not for the exact identity. The resulting \(\ell\) weight for two cells is at most \(E^{-2}\) after taking the common subpower out, so it is summable. The normalized \(L'\) term, after division by \(D=F_0 ZL\), is \(O(L/F_0)\) up to subpowers, with no signed gain claimed there.

Combining the other terms gives \(F_0^{-1} Z^{-(1-\sigma)}\) times their usual monomials at \(L\). Sum \(F_0^{-1}\) over the frozen cells in their own dyads using elementary counting, and sum the logarithmically many dyads; \(Z\ge F^{1/2}\), \(L\ll D/F\), \(F>Y/16\). This proves (1) for each pairing with that exponent (and \(Y^{-(1-\sigma)}\) for \(\mu\)–\(\mu\)); combine by \(A_U=G-\mu\).

## 5. Critical powers and exact remaining scope

At \(H=D^{3/4}\), \(Y=D^y\), use the conditional \(7/8+\eta\) input and absorb smaller \(\eta\) into \(\epsilon\). The four powers in (1) are \(3/4-y/16,\ 1-y,\ 23/24-(19/48)y,\ 13/12-(43/48)y\). They are all at most \(3/4\) at \(y=10/19\). At \(y=11/20\) the maximum is \(237/320\), a margin \(3/320\) below \(3/4\), up to the stated subpower. Elementary \(\sigma=1\) still only gives sufficient cutoff \(D^{5/8}\). The conditional sharp-cutoff \(D^{4/7}\) in the preceding note remains distinct. The sharp product indicator has a jump; its log Fourier transform does not have the required weighted absolute integral. This proof does not give the two-cell gain uniformly through that sharp limit or separately bound the difference from a literal tail with cutoff \(Y\).

The result is for full intermediate \(d\), with rough primes, not the original column gcd or the smooth-only \(K_0\) tail. It leaves the complementary small intermediate-divisor weight, including \(d=1\), coherent \(K_0-\mathcal J\), and the far-norm Gauss sector open. The whole-prefix candidate \(109/144\), whole signed \(13/12\), global unsigned \(9/8\), short/principal assembly, determinant and zero-location by scope, Lean and GRH remain unchanged.
