# Asymmetric insertion and erasure at small intermediate divisors

8 November 2026 UTC. Use the exact selection, masks, two-family separation, and notation of TWO_FAMILY_CROSS-v1. In particular \(B,K\) are the dyadic physical norms of cutoff erasure and increasing-frequency factors, \(E=Nd\), and \(\Phi(A,T)=A+T+A^{5/6}T^{1/3}+A^{1/3}T^{5/6}\). The estimates have the common \((DH)^\epsilon\mathcal M_{16}(W)^2\) cost and fixed-data constants of that note. They keep all \(q_\ell\) erasures of old exponent one or two and arbitrary good residue changes on \(d\).

Use \(y_0^{-\alpha}(y'_1)^{-\theta}\), with \(\alpha+\theta=3/2\) and \(1/3\le\theta\le1\). Keep the additional \((1+K/R)^{-(4-\theta)}\) from the actual primal-zero test on \(y'_1\). Multiplying the exact scalar by these powers leaves \(R^{3/2}/(\sqrt D\sqrt E)\), times \((B/K)^\theta\) and the label weight
\[
 \frac{Nc\,E_0^{\alpha-1}E_1^{\theta-1}(Nj/N\widetilde q_d)^\theta
       (N\mathfrak e_\ell)^\theta}
 {Ng\,N\ell\,Nz_*\,Nf\,(Na_0)^{6\theta-1}(Nb)^2
        (N\ell_0 Nv)^{6\theta}(Nk_S)^\theta}.
\]
This follows from the same product of the two exact scalar weights used in (6) of TWO_FAMILY_CROSS-v1. The \(f\)-sum is harmonic. The \(a_0\)-exponent is at least one. Pay \(Nc\) by \(c\mid z_*\), disjoint from the \(q_\ell\) and conjugate charges by \(q_\ell\perp a\) and \((\ell,\bar\ell)=1\). Pay \(Nj\) in the harmonic \(\ell\)-count using \(\bar j\mid\ell\) to the required power at most one. The \(q_\ell\) weights are harmonic or better through \(\theta=1\) using the third charge on the old exponent-two state, exactly as in TWO_FAMILY_CROSS-v1. The only potentially growing \(d\)-dependent factor is \(E_0^{\max(0,\alpha-1)}\ll_S E^{\max(0,\alpha-1)}\). The \(S\)-part of \(e_{1,0}\) is bounded. This gives, after the exact two-family comparison, the per-\(d\) bound
\[
 \frac{R^{3/2}E^{-1/2+\max(0,\alpha-1)}}{\sqrt D\,A_1}
 (B/K)^{\theta-1/2}\sqrt{\Phi(A_1,B)\Phi(A_1,K)}
 (1+K/R)^{-(4-\theta)}.                                             \tag{1}
\]

For \(K\ge B\), use \(\theta=1\). Since \(\Phi(A_1,K)/K\) is nonincreasing, the sieve and ratio factors cost at most \(\Phi(A_1,B)\). The remaining \((1+K/R)^{-3}\) controls \(B>R\), again using degree at most one in the second sieve argument. This gives at most the value of (1) at \(B=K=R\), up to fixed constants. For \(K\le B\le R\), use \(\theta=1/3\), so the ratio factor is at most one, and bound the sieve factors by \(\Phi(A_1,R)\).

For the remaining \(K<B,\ B>R\), use \(\theta=1/3\) again. Monotonicity below \(R\) and degree at most one above \(R\) give
\[
 K^{1/6}\sqrt{\Phi(A_1,K)}(1+K/R)^{-11/3}
 \ll R^{1/6}\sqrt{\Phi(A_1,R)}.
\]
It remains to maximize \(B^{-1/6}\sqrt{\Phi(A_1,B)}\) for \(R\ll B\ll D\). Its square is a sum of positive monomials, so comparison at \(R,D\) suffices by convexity in \(\log B\), up to fixed dyadic constants. At the exact mixed scales \(A_1\asymp D^{5/12}\), \(A_2\asymp D^{1/6}\), \(R\asymp D^{7/12}\), one has \(\Phi(A_1,R)\ll D^{5/8}\) and \(\Phi(A_1,D)\ll D\). The \(D\)-powers in (1) at \(B=R,D\), before the divisor weight, are respectively \(7/12\) and \(101/144\). The remaining divisor weight is at most \(E^{-1/3}\). Thus all \(B,K\) in the selected mixed cross meet the \(D^{3/4+\epsilon}\) target on \(Nd\le D^{7/96}\), since the sum of \((Nd)^{-1/3}\) costs at most \(D^{(7/96)(2/3)}\). The comparable slice retains the stronger \(Nd\le D^{1/3}\) conclusion of TWO_FAMILY_CROSS-v1.

For the pure unit divisor, the first two cases above give target throughout \(K\ge B\) or \(B\le R\). The pure wedge \(B>R,\ K<B\) is only partially covered by the dyadic comparisons. These estimates do not supply the cancellation in the intermediate divisor needed for the general signed sum.
