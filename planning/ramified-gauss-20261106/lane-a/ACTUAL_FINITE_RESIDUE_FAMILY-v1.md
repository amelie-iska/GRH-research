## 3. Joint local selectors and primitive zeros

A joint finite local selector need not itself be multiplicative. Before freezing a primitive type, express its ACTUAL bounded function \(f(h_m,h_n)\) on the appropriate fixed product residue group by the FULL product-character Fourier expansion. The finite sum reconstructs the zero exactly; no Fourier term is individually advertised as an original nonzero character row. Primitive conductors are not enlarged for evaluation, and dropped conductor primes do not restore original raw zeros.

More explicitly, let \(\kappa_{\rm prim}\) induce one resulting fixed finite character and let \(F_{\rm raw}\) contain its primitive conductor, all stipulated fixed exclusions and all fixed numerator supports. Its actual denominator function is
\[

Equation (1) is realized as an imprimitive finite residue character on the fixed modulus \(F_{\rm raw}\); it need not be primitive, which the full finite-Fourier/rational-cusp construction allows.
