# String classifications, surface models and completed dessin orders

Read-only primary-source scope review, 2026-11-03. No shared manuscript, source clone, environment or generated project output was changed. This report supplies two direct algebraic lemmas and an actual smooth quotient to complement the scope corrections.

## Precise primary statements

**Crawley-Boevey**, [Classification of modules for infinite-dimensional string algebras, arXiv:1308.6410v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.6410v2), 4 March2016, 25pp: definitions pp1–4; Theorems1.1–1.4 p4; completion discussion p5; Propositions12.1–12.2 pp24–25. A string algebra may have infinite dimension or infinitely many vertices; its relations are monomial zero relations and permitted continuations are unique. A module is finitely controlled when each vertex space lies in a finitely generated submodule; pointwise artinian means every descending submodule chain stabilizes at each vertex. Theorem1.2 decomposes finitely controlled modules into strings and finite-dimensional bands. Theorem1.3 adds primitive injective bands for pointwise artinian modules. Theorem1.4 gives uniqueness. Words may be finite, one-sided or two-sided infinite. Bands use periodic words and indecomposable Laurent-polynomial coefficient modules. Primitive injective bands have direct/inverse periodic words and injective-envelope coefficients. Propositions12.1–12.2 impose eventual-tail and support finiteness conditions; arbitrary infinite words do not automatically satisfy either theorem. The p5 proposed adaptation to completions is prospective, not a proved theorem there.

**Opper–Plamondon–Schroll**, [A geometric model for the derived category of gentle algebras, arXiv:1801.09659v7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09659v7), 10July2025 (PDF dated11July), 62pp. Conventions p5 and Definition1.7 p8 assume finite-dimensional graded gentle algebras. The principal category is thick(simples), identified with D_fd through the cited Booth–Goodbody–Opper work; Perf is a subcategory. In grading zero D_fd is the bounded derived category of finite-dimensional modules. Definition1.19 pp12–13 and Theorem2.13 pp21–22 admit specified infinite arcs spiralling around unmarked boundary components counterclockwise. Bands need graded non-exceptional closed curves and coefficient data. Theorem3.3 p27 uses oriented graded intersections, with exceptional coincident closed-curve identity and AR connecting maps. Thus a naive ungraded crossing count is insufficient. Theorem2.13 is not a classification of arbitrary modules over an infinite-dimensional completion. AppendixC explicitly discusses the Koszul-dual smooth but potentially infinite-dimensional algebra; that does not remove the main algebra's stated hypotheses.

**Lekili–Polishchuk**, [Derived equivalences of gentle algebras via Fukaya categories, arXiv:1801.06370v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.06370v5), 27August2019, 35pp. Definition3.1.1 p12 deliberately permits infinite-dimensional locally gentle algebras. D(A) here means perfect dg modules, not all bounded modules (Remark3.1.2 p13). Lemma3.1.3 p13 characterizes smoothness by absence of forbidden cycles, and properness by absence of permitted cycles. Theorem3.2.2 pp17–18 gives Perf_dg(A) equivalent to the partially wrapped Fukaya category for homologically smooth A, with a specified grading, line field and stops on a surface with nonempty boundary. The proof constructs generating arcs whose complementary disks each contain one stop. Remark3.3.5 p22 proposes an infinitesimally wrapped replacement for nonsmooth proper A and expressly leaves generation unchecked. It is not that missing theorem. Their references point to [Haiden–Katzarkov–Kontsevich, arXiv:1409.8611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.8611); this review uses LP's explicit theorem/proof rather than claiming a full fresh audit of HKK.

## Direct application to the polynomial dessin algebra

For a finite quiver, finitely controlled is equivalent to finitely generated: choose for each of the finitely many vertices a finitely generated submodule containing its whole vertex space, then sum them. Conversely the module itself suffices. Likewise pointwise artinian is equivalent to artinian: for a descending chain choose a common stabilization index for the finitely many vertex spaces.

Our polynomial node A=k[x,y]/(xy) is exactly one of Crawley-Boevey's examples, so every finitely generated A-module receives the Theorem1.2 classification, and every artinian A-module receives Theorem1.3. More generally every closed polynomial dessin algebra here satisfies that paper's string-algebra conditions: finite quiver, at most two incoming/outgoing arrows and unique permitted predecessor/successor. Thus its finitely generated and artinian modules fall under those statements too. Opposite-module/travel conventions must be translated if using our right-module convention.

This is a real classification for infinite-dimensional algebras, not a reduction to a finite-dimensional replacement. It does not classify every module. Infinite words can already occur for finitely generated modules. The ordinary regular node A is generated by1 and has vector-space basis 1,x,x^2,...,y,y^2,..., with both branch actions directed away from1. Its word presentation therefore has two infinite tails despite finite generation. Word length, vector-space dimension and number of module generators are different quantities.

For a finite quiver, the 'right vertex-finite' alternative in Proposition12.1 cannot sustain an infinite tail: some vertex repeats infinitely often. Hence its finitely generated decomposition has finitely many summands with the eventual-inverse condition on both ends in the author's convention. The artinian analogue has eventual-direct ends. One must not replace those conditions with 'all infinite words'.

## Elementary lemma: completed lattices escape both finiteness classes

Let A be our polynomial node, u=x+y, R=k[[u]], and Lambda its arrow-adic completion. Let M be a nonzero Lambda-module finite free over R. Then M, restricted to A, is neither finitely generated nor artinian.

Proof. A is finite free on1,x over k[u], with x^2=ux. If M were finitely generated over A it would be finitely generated over k[u]. Every nonzero polynomial p(u) is u^m times a unit in R, so it acts injectively on the R-free M. Thus M would be a nonzero finite torsion-free k[u]-module, hence finite free over the PID k[u]. Multiplication by1-u cannot be surjective on such a module: its cokernel has dimension equal to its positive rank over k. But1-u is a unit of R and therefore acts bijectively on M. Contradiction. Finally M contains the strictly descending chain M > uM > u^2M > ... because R-freeness gives nonzero M/uM. Hence M is not artinian.

Consequently restriction of any nonzero completed node lattice satisfies neither Crawley-Boevey theorem's finiteness hypothesis. This is not merely a lack of a checked citation. The same argument applies to any of our closed orders when the polynomial algebra is finite over its central k[u]: every sufficiently long path is u times a shorter path, giving finite generation over k[u]. It also applies independently of the explicit node lattice classification.

This does not prevent separate classification of completed lattices. The completed node has the independently proved lattice decomposition Lambda^r + X^a + Y^b and its two genuine lattice AR sequences. That is a theorem in its exact category, not a direct consequence of restricting the polynomial string theorem.

## Concrete counterexample to unrestricted string-and-band classification

Take M=k[[x]], with x acting by multiplication and y acting by zero, as an ordinary polynomial-node A-module. It is a rank-one R-free completed lattice (u acts as x).

Every A-endomorphism F of M preserves x^n M. For f in M and its degree<n polynomial truncation f_n, F(f)-f_n F(1) belongs to x^n M. Taking all n and using separatedness gives F(f)=f F(1). Thus End_A(M)=k[[x]], a local ring, so M is indecomposable.

Nevertheless M is not any ordinary Crawley-Boevey string or band. If y acts as zero in a word module, its word cannot contain y or y^{-1}: either occurrence induces a nonzero partial basis shift. A nonperiodic word using only x^{±1} cannot change direction, since immediate inverse cancellation is forbidden. It therefore gives a finite x-string, the one-sided free ray k[x], or the opposite locally-nilpotent ray. A two-sided such word is periodic, hence is not a string under that definition. M is infinite-dimensional and x is injective but not surjective, excluding the finite and locally-nilpotent cases. It is not k[x], since1-x is bijective on M but not on k[x]. In a periodic pure-x band, x is invertible, for every nonzero Laurent coefficient module. Thus M is not a band either. Allowing arbitrary indecomposable Laurent coefficient modules does not repair the claim.

Completion replaces finite-support basis sums by genuine formal-series behavior. An ordinary infinite string module has an algebraic direct-sum basis; it is not its completed product. This difference is visible even in this simplest dessin.

## What the surface/Fukaya bridge actually gives

A grading on arrows produces a dg algebra with zero differential. Perf_dg(A) is generated by finite cones, shifts and direct summands of A. This differs from the derived category of graded modules, where internal and cohomological shifts are separate. In degree zero Perf(A)=K^b(proj_fg A). For a finite-dimensional algebra of finite global dimension it equals D^b(mod_fd A). Without finite global dimension, residue simples may have infinite projective resolutions and lie outside Perf. For a smooth infinite-dimensional algebra, Perf can already contain infinite-dimensional modules, so it cannot be silently identified with D^b(mod_fd A).

Every nonempty closed dessin in our construction has a permitted-successor permutation and a forbidden-successor permutation on a nonempty finite dart set. Both have cycles. By LP's criterion, its polynomial algebra is neither proper nor homologically smooth. Thus two different obstacles occur: the OPS main theorem's finite-dimensionality is absent, and the LP theorem permits infinite dimension but its smoothness hypothesis fails. Merely invoking the word 'gentle' does not settle either issue. For the ordinary finite-type smooth partially wrapped categories in the LP construction, dg Morita equivalence preserves smoothness; consequently their direct standard Perf model cannot identify our nonsmooth algebra with one of those smooth models. This does not prohibit singular/infinitesimal/topological/completed variants with their own precise statements.

A literal positive quotient bridge exists. Set y=0 in the polynomial node. B=A/(y)=k[x] is a connected locally gentle algebra with one permitted loop and no forbidden cycle. LP's theorem applies to Perf(k[x]) in any chosen arrow grading. In grading zero, k[x] is noetherian of global dimension1, so this is also D^b(mod_fg k[x]), not merely finite-dimensional modules. Its surface has Euler characteristic |Q0|-|Q1|=0, hence is an annulus; the single forbidden thread gives one stop total, leaving one boundary component unmarked. This provides an actual partially wrapped category attached to a specified polynomial quotient of the dessin algebra.

The bridge remains intrinsic to that quotient. Restriction from B-modules to A-modules is fully faithful on the ordinary abelian categories but does not preserve perfectness or derived full faithfulness: B has the A-resolution with alternating y,x maps. Applying Hom_A(-,B) gives cochain maps0,x,0,x,..., so Ext_A^2(B,B)=B/xB=k, while Ext_B^2(B,B)=0. In particular B is perfect over itself but not over A. The corresponding completed quotient is k[[x]], which is not the same polynomial algebra and needs a separate topological/completed category theorem.

For the genuine finite-dimensional H_n quotient categories already constructed in the dessin project, OPS applies intrinsically to their ordinary bounded derived categories. Their endpoint square-zero loops give forbidden cycles, so this does not itself make them smooth LP examples. Neither that quotient theorem nor an ordinary exact inflation automatically extends to the whole completed order's derived category.

## Tiling and infinity wording suitable for an overview

A surface dissection produces a gentle or locally gentle algebra only after specifying the admissible arcs, vertices, cyclic order, relation rule and finiteness conditions. Necessary checks include two-in/two-out bounds and uniqueness of both permitted and forbidden successors/predecessors. An arbitrary cell tiling, quiver drawing or polygon adjacency algebra need not satisfy those conditions. Our closed clean dessins do satisfy the locally gentle conditions; their permitted cycles specifically prevent finite-dimensional gentleness under the OPS convention. Terminology is not uniform: LP calls this larger class gentle.

Infinite-word ends in the algebraic classification encode tail behavior and finiteness conditions. In OPS's specific derived surface model, infinite homotopy-string ends have prescribed counterclockwise spiralling around an unmarked boundary component; replacing that component by an endpoint compactification produces the puncture picture. This is neither an arbitrary boundary condition nor every infinite word from every module category. Ordinary strings, homotopy strings of complexes, and completed formal modules must be distinguished. Closed curves require grading and local-system/band parameters; formal inverse letters are symbols encoding reverse traversal, not inverses of algebra arrows.

A reliable overview can say: 'Polynomial locally gentle dessin algebras have string/band classifications for specified finitely generated or artinian module categories; completed lattices require their own order-theoretic classification. For finite-dimensional graded gentle algebras, the cited surface model describes D_fd, including certain infinite homotopy strings. For homologically smooth, possibly infinite-dimensional graded locally gentle algebras, the cited theorem identifies perfect dg modules with a graded partially wrapped Fukaya category. Our closed orders require additional singular/completed categorical constructions.'

## Final manuscript incorporation review

Read all of `papers/dessin-string-scope.tex` and the full final section of `papers/dessin-gluing-details.tex` (lines69–80 at review). The scope text correctly states direct applicability to finitely generated/artinian polynomial modules, the failure of both hypotheses for all nonzero completed R-free lattices, the elementary formal branch counterexample, and the distinction between the full closed order and an intrinsic smooth quotient category. The ordinary module versus derived inflation distinction is explicit.

The k[[x]] counterexample covers the full ordinary word list, including length-zero/trivial strings: a nonzero finite pure-x string has nilpotent x and hence nonzero kernel. The opposite ray is locally nilpotent, whereas x is injective on k[[x]]. The other ray is k[x], on which1-x is not onto. Every pure-x periodic band has invertible x because its Laurent coefficient parameter is invertible, independently of finite dimension, injective-envelope/artinian type, or any other indecomposable coefficient module. A y-letter or inverse y-letter gives a nonzero arrow action on the corresponding basis/coefficient block, so cannot occur when y=0. Thus none of the stated counterexample cases is silently omitted.

The general closed-A lemma is justified by the actual central element u whose branch coordinates are (u_c I)_c. Every sufficiently long path is u times a shorter nonempty path on the same permitted branch. Repetition reduces to finitely many paths of bounded length and the vertex idempotents, proving A finite over k[u] even when full turns have unequal path degrees. On an R-free lattice every nonzero k[u] polynomial is injective because it is u^m times a formal unit. The finite-free PID conclusion, failure of1-u surjectivity on a positive-rank finite free k[u]-module, and strictly descending u-adic chain are all valid.

The annulus model and one-stop count are correct, but a literal source-convention correction was sent to root: details78 calls the thread 'trivial'. It is the **length-one forbidden thread x**, not a trivial forbidden thread. LP Definition3.1.4 only includes the trivial forbidden thread at a one-in/one-out vertex when the two-arrow composite is forbidden; here x^2 is permitted. The single-arrow thread x cannot extend as a forbidden word and is maximal. It gives exactly one stop. LP's surface has Euler characteristic zero and nonempty boundary, so it is an annulus, with one boundary unmarked and one bearing that stop. The correction changes no category or numerical conclusion.

The alternating y,x resolution of B=A/(y) gives Hom cochain maps0,x,0,x,..., hence Ext_A^(2r)(B,B)=k for r>=1 and all odd positive self-Ext vanish. Over B itself every positive self-Ext of B vanishes. Thus derived inflation is demonstrably not fully faithful, and the generator B does not remain perfect over A. In grading zero the PID/noetherian argument gives finite projective resolutions for all finitely generated B-modules and proves the stated equality Perf(B)=D^b(mod_fg B). The completed branch is expressly a different category.
