## Exponents and assembly

If \(k\ge b\) and \(k\le k_0\), use (1) with \(\theta=1\). Monotonicity allows replacing \(b\) by \(k\) in the positive comparison. Its exponent is at most \(r+\Delta(k)/2+1/40-3(k-r)_+\). This is maximal at \(k=r\) since the slope of \(\Delta\) is at most one, yielding \(r+5/48+1/40=57/80\). If \(k\ge b,\ k>k_0\), the same choice gives \(r+\Delta(k)-3(k-r)\), decreasing for \(k\ge k_0\), hence at most \(101/144<57/80\).

If \(k\le b\le r\), use (1) with \(\theta=1/6\) and the sharp insertion bound: \((K/B)^{1/3}\) and the retained decay are at most one, so the same \(57/80\) holds. If \(k\le b,\ b\ge r,\ k\le k_0\), use (4) and the live-support cap (2). The power after summing \(d\) is at most
\[
 23/48+\Delta(b)/2+1/40+(k-b)/4+(1-b)/4-(15/4)(k-r)_+ .
\]
At fixed \(b\ge r\) this is maximal at \(k=r\). The resulting expression equals \(57/80\) at \(b=r\), decreases with slope \(-1/12\) up to \(5/6\), and is constant thereafter. Finally if \(k_0<k\le b\) use (1) with \(\theta=1/6\) and both ordinary moments. The exponent at fixed \(b\) is decreasing in \(k\ge k_0\), because the \(\Delta(k)/2\) slope is at most \(1/2\) and the retained decay has slope \(23/6\). At \(k=k_0\) the function of \(b\) is convex, with maximal endpoint value \(\max(2/3,205/288)=205/288<57/80\) on \([k_0,1]\).
