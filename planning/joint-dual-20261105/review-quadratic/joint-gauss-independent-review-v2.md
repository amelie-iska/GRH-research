# Independent review of the joint five-power Gauss reduction, v2

Verdict: **accepted**, as an exact finite completion and orientation norm reduction. This review covers the frozen v2 research note, compact TeX proposals and the corresponding production bytes. It supplies no incomplete-moment estimate, global power gain, Lean theorem or GRH conclusion.

## Exact phase and masks

For good squarefree m=hr and n=ht, h,r,t are pairwise coprime, q=rt is squarefree and nonunit off the coefficient diagonal. On the finite element ring O/q the raw theta=x_r conjugate(x_t) has local exponent +1 or -1. Thus each theta^j, 1<=j<=5, is primitive on q, and theta^3=x_q^3=xi_q independently of orientation. This is not asserted for arbitrary four-column exponents. All primes cancelled from the conductor remain deleted by the h mask, including in the sixth-power variable's full L=hq mask. The arbitrary sixth row factor may overlap the first five.

For the additive character e_beta(z)=exp(2 pi i Tr(z/(delta beta))), delta=1+2 omega generates the different. The trace dual of O is delta^(-1)O. Consequently the annihilator of O in this pairing is exactly (beta), proving nondegeneracy on O/q and the correct ell^(-6) six-variable inversion factor. This argument applies to split and inert prime ideals and makes no rational-norm-squarefree assumption.

CRT writes the q-component additive frequency as k alpha^(-1), giving

    G_j(k)=theta(alpha)^j tau_beta(theta^j) conjugate(theta(k)^j) c_h(k)

for q-unit k, and zero otherwise. The h-component's frequency is k beta^(-1); since beta is an h-unit, its Ramanujan sum is c_h(k). The sixth transform is c_L(k). Each h factor therefore survives with its actual possibly signed Ramanujan value.

The two standard finite Gauss pairings give

    tau(theta)tau(theta^5)=theta(-1)Q,
    tau(theta^2)tau(theta^4)=theta(-1)^2Q.

Together with the remaining tau(theta^3) and theta(alpha)^(1+2+3+4+5)=xi_q(alpha), this gives exactly Q^2 xi_q(-alpha) tau_beta(xi_q), with the remaining orientation character conjugate(theta(product k_j^j)). All signs and conjugations in GB and the compact TeX equation are correct.

The v1 sentence specializing the prefactor at h=1 was incomplete under arbitrary unit generators: for q of norm7, alpha=-1 need not have xi_q(alpha)=1. V2 explicitly chooses alpha=1 when h=1 and resolves that issue while preserving v1 bytes. More generally beta->v beta scales tau_beta(eta) by eta(v), while alpha->u alpha and beta->v beta rescale full-modulus frequencies by (uv)^(-1). Since the weighted frequency product has total exponent15, the character factor transforms by xi_q(uv), exactly matching the prefactor. Thus no unrecorded generator phase is being suppressed.

## S, units, ray conditions and the actual coefficients

This reduction works directly with original residue characters on the element lattice. It does not replace them by unit-trivial ideal Hecke characters and hence does not require a new finite S-twist/ray-family comparison. The fixed literal u0 consists of a unit and an S-supported factor; it is coprime to the good L. Its orientation-dependent value theta(u0) remains in a(r). All primary/ray, squarefree, inter-factor, exact capacity and common row-profile conditions remain in the exact finite F. No assumption that F factors, is smooth, or has independent row variables occurs. An orientation-dependent restriction cannot be hidden in this common F for orientation Parseval; v2 states that caveat.

The coefficient identity mu(hr)mu(ht)=mu(q) is exact. The fixed nu(h) cancels against its conjugate because good ideals are outside its conductor and nu is unitary there. Both actual factor counts r_U and both possibly complex common W factors stay in a(r), with excluded orientations padded by zero. For the mixed C-versus-mu term exactly the second r_U factor is replaced by1. Thus JD remains an identity for the original signed outer conductor sum; it has not discarded selected sectors by positivity.

## Orientation norm and the remaining obstruction

Distinct orientations differ at a nonempty set of primes. The quotient character has exponent +/-2 there, hence is a nontrivial cubic character; CRT unit orthogonality proves OP with phi(q), including coefficients padded by zero. Taking |tau_beta(xi_q)|=sqrt(Q) and ordinary Cauchy gives the stated Q^(5/2)/(D ell^6) finite bound.

The product map from five q-unit frequencies has exactly phi(q)^4 points in each fiber because k1 is uniquely determined by the other four. All orientations agree precisely on the cubic-residue coherent set x_p(K)^2=1 for all p|q, of size phi(q)/3^omega(q). These facts do not supply a bound for the actual Fourier/Ramanujan weight B. They give no pointwise orientation cancellation on that coherent set.

Centered representatives in the lattice generated by alpha beta and alpha beta omega have absolute value <=|alpha beta|=sqrt(ell), so completion has the claimed finite height. Replacing B by B_Omega gives the stated exact Cauchy error bound for a common frequency subset. Nothing here proves a small tail below this height for the exact sharp F.

At the pure A3 endpoint, inverse transforms of the literal unit variables restore their fixed values and leave the orientation-independent xi_q character. The note's endpoint obstruction and the partial three-power Jacobi identity are correct. Completing five powers is therefore not a proof of a near-face or global incomplete-moment saving.
