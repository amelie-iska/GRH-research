# Optimization limit for separate physical moments on the balanced edge

8 November 2026 UTC. Author derivation, independent review pending. In the scope of HIGHER_EQUALITY_MOMENTS-v1, consider the limiting pure edge \(A_1\asymp R\asymp D^{3/4}\), \(A_2\asymp1\), \(E=Nd\asymp1\), \(L_1,L_2\asymp D^{1/2}\) and the \(w=1\) term of the exact common-prime expansion. Here constant-scale \(m,n\) must be nonunit if there is any primal support. The original strict lower cutoff makes the literal \(d=1\) sector empty. Keep all original good squarefree labels, phases, one complex \(W\), fixed row family and exact equality compensation. This is a statement about powers delivered by one upper-bound strategy at these scales.

Use the equality pointwise bound and all fixed even equality moments from the product argument of HIGHER_EQUALITY_MOMENTS-v1. For the two cutoff arrays use their elementary pointwise counts and all fixed even moments obtained by applying the physical sixth-order second moment to products. In the \(2k\)-th cutoff moment, group the \(k\)-th power by actual good ideal product \(b\), of length \(O_k(L_i^k)\), with divisor-bounded coefficients. Split \(b=b_0 q^6\) uniquely into a sixth-free part and the sixth power. At fixed \(q\) the exact raw factor is \(\psi_u(q)^6\), with its twist and zeros. The residual mass is \(O((DH)^\epsilon L_i^k/(Nq)^6)\). Apply the physical sieve on the shortened \(b_0\) length and sum \(q\) by the triangle with \((Nq)^{-3}\), using direct counting on bounded nonempty subunit lengths. Taking the \(k\)-th root gives the cutoff moment comparison with excess power \(\Delta_6(k/2)/(2k)\) above its \(\sqrt{L_i}\) normalization. The bound for \(k=1\) is the original second moment. The construction invokes no larger-order sieve.

Write \(x\) for a reciprocal row-norm exponent, with \(x=0\) for the pointwise comparison. At \(x=1/(2j)\), the equality excess above \(\sqrt{D/E}\) from the same cubic product comparison is
\[
 f_E(x)=-\tfrac1{16}+x\Delta_3(1/(16x))
       \ \ge\ -\tfrac1{48}-\tfrac x4 .
\]
The supporting line follows from the cubic mixed monomial in \(\Delta_3\); it remains below the \(x=0\) pointwise excess zero. The formula at \(j=1\) follows from the grouped cubic second moment. At \(x=1/(2k)\) each cutoff moment excess is
\[
 f_B(x)=x\Delta_6(1/(4x))
       \ \ge\ \tfrac7{48}-\tfrac x4 .
\]
For \(k=1\) equality is exact. For \(k\ge2\), the sixth-order mixed monomial gives \(f_B(x)\ge 5/24-x/2\), which is at least \(7/48-x/4\) because \(x\le1/4\). At \(k=2\) equality is again exact. The \(x=0\) cutoff count has excess \(1/4\), above the same line. Any interpolation between these individual comparisons preserves the two supporting-line bounds on their quoted exponents. Any alternative trivial positive moment bound from the same pointwise comparisons also remains above those lines.

In separate row Hölder, \(x_E+x_1+x_2=1\). Summing the supporting lines gives excess at least
\[
 -\tfrac1{48}+2\cdot\tfrac7{48}-\tfrac14=\tfrac1{48}.
\]
The equality sixth moment at \(x_E=1/6\), one cutoff second moment at \(x_1=1/2\) and the other cutoff third moment at \(x_2=1/3\), interpolated from its second and fourth, attain this value. Thus within this separate-moment comparison, adding higher even moments and all their interpolations cannot reduce the uniform quoted excess below \(1/48\) at the balanced edge. This conclusion concerns the monomials used as upper comparisons. It says nothing about a lower bound for a single actual cross, norm or frequency contribution. It leaves open sharper sparse moments, joint row products, use of signed coefficients before Cauchy, and any cancellation with the original compensation.
