# Two exact coprimalities in the selected wide unit-pair modes

\[
 (\mathfrak k,z_0)=(\mathfrak k,\bar z_0)=1 .
\]
Keep the same allowable bounded frequency weight and original windows as in that note. This is an additional condition on the actual partner terms. It uses both ideal orientations, including any prime of the conjugate that belongs to \(S\). The three low-frequency thresholds \(19/44,83/176,45/88\) of that note hold unchanged with this coupled selection. The selected middle equality term is exactly empty. No conclusion here concerns an insertion and erasure selection with arbitrary overlap.

Fix the original \(d,m,n,r,e_0,e_1,J\) and the ordered \(e_0\)-allocation. As in SELECTED_LOW_FREQUENCIES-v2 write \(\zeta_i=e_{0,i}a_i\), so \(z_0=a_1a_2\), with the \(a_i\) good squarefree, mutually coprime and individually satisfying their fixed deletions. In particular the \(a_i\) avoid \(d,r,e_0\). Before expanding their mutual coprimality, insert the four exact projectors
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^2
    \left(\sum_{s_i\mid(a_i,\mathfrak k)}\mu(s_i)\right)
    \left(\sum_{t_i\mid(a_i,\bar{\mathfrak k})}\mu(t_i)\right).
\]
The \(s_i,t_i\) are eligible good squarefree divisors of \(a_i\), tested before stripping. Put
\[
 w_i=[s_i,t_i],\qquad
 s=s_1s_2,\quad t=t_1t_2,\qquad V=[s,\bar t],
\]
where brackets denote ideal least common multiples. Because the original \(a_i\) are coprime, \(w_1\perp w_2\). Conversely at fixed disjoint \(w_i\), writing \(a_i=w_i c_i\), require each squarefree \(c_i\) to avoid \(w_1w_2\) and all original fixed deletions. The remaining mutual coprimality is \((c_1,c_2)=1\). Expand it by the exact common squarefree \(w\mid(c_1,c_2)\) with original eligibility before stripping, and put \(c_i=w b_i\). The two \(b_i\) individually avoid \(w,w_1,w_2\) and their fixed deleted union. These successive expansions recover the original coprimalities and cancel every other tuple on their respective extended arrays.

The frequency divisibility is exactly \(V\mid\mathfrak k\), so write \(\mathfrak k=V\mathfrak k'\). There is no deletion between \(V,\mathfrak k'\), which may overlap because the original frequency can repeat primes. The conjugate \(\bar t\) and hence \(V\) may meet \(S\), with their original raw values. The full fixed phase from this shortening is
\(\psi_u(w_1w_2)\overline{\psi_u(V)}\), together with the original \(\psi_u(w)^2\) and all unchanged row phases and signs. It is not generally a squared modulus. In particular use a least common multiple for \(V\) even if an ideal prime occurs both in \(s\) and in \(\bar t\). The three separated variable arrays use the literal phases on \(b_1,b_2,\mathfrak k'\).

 \frac1{Nw\sqrt{Nw_1\,Nw_2\,NV}}
   =\frac1{Nw\sqrt{N[s,t]\,N[s,\bar t]}}

relative to the product of the unshortened square-root masses with the fixed \(e_0\)-partition. Indeed \(w_1w_2=[s,t]\) by the original disjoint assignments. The \(w\)-sum is harmonic. To sum the other weight, group the finite local states in \(s_i,t_i\) by the rational prime underneath them. For a split prime \(p\), there are two labeled ideal primes of norm \(p\). If either appears in \(s\) or \(t\), then both \([s,t]\) and \([s,\bar t]\) have a prime above \(p\), although the labels may differ. Thus the local weight is at most \(p^{-1}\), with an absolute bounded number of possibilities for the presence and cutoff assignment bits. An inert prime has one prime ideal of norm \(p^2\) and gives at most \(p^{-2}\). The ramified prime is excluded by the fixed \(S\). The union support has polynomial norm, since \(s,t\mid z_0\), \(Nz_0\le D\). For a squarefree rational support \(P\) the number of states is at most \(C^{\omega(P)}\) for an absolute \(C\). The sum of these weights is \(O_\epsilon(D^\epsilon)\) by the divisor bound and harmonic counting. This proves the same positive three-array estimate, retaining the original outer sign until the absolute divisor comparison.

Finally the two coprimalities imply \(\gcd_{\mathbb Z}(Nz_0,N\mathfrak k)=1\). For a split rational prime dividing \(Nz_0\), every ideal prime above it is in \(z_0\) or \(\bar z_0\); for an inert prime the single label suffices. The same reasoning applies to any ramified norm common to the two, and the actual cutoff is good. Thus equality of the two positive norms forces both to be one. The original middle test is \(K_0<N\mathfrak k\le MD^\eta\), with \(K_0\ge1\) as stated in LOW_DUAL_CUTOFF-v1, §Setting and exact split, and \(K_0=1\) in its pure selection. Therefore the exact middle equality subtraction is empty under these two selected masks.
