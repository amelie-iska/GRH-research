\chapter*{The program and its standard of proof}
A finite presentation becomes scientifically useful when its realizations are explicit. This volume develops that principle across gentle relations, integral orders, BGG structures, graph-to-graph computation, quantum spin systems, nonlinear dynamics and information-bearing observations. The common language is constructive: identify the object, specify the map, prove the preserved identity and measure the error introduced by approximation.

The volume includes a source-linked archive of the underlying research proofs, a direct uniform graph-approximation construction, selected Lean formalizations, independently executed CPU/browser/GPU circuits, exact finite spectral and channel realizations, and measured-data experiments with complete coding costs. Positive results are stated as results. Conditional transports retain their premises. Empirical comparisons retain every outcome. These are the rules that let a broad program accumulate knowledge rather than merely accumulate analogies.

The earlier 37-page supplement and its measured results remain reproducible historical artifacts. This edition integrates the new proofs, controls and visual workbench while preserving their provenance. The central surface explorer is available at \url{https://amelie-iska.github.io/GRH-research/workbench/surface.html}; its export binds the displayed configuration to the actual computed observations.
